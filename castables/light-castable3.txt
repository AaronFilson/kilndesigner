Yes, I have direct access to global patent data and historical recipes for manufacturing insulating fire bricks (IFBs). The patent records detail precise chemical formulations, pore-forming mechanisms (burnout materials), and binding systems used to achieve high thermal resistance and lightweight structural integrity.
Patent literature classifies these recipes by their specific raw materials, binding chemistry, and structural pore-forming agents:

1. Traditional Kaolin & Organic Burnout Recipes

Historically, standard commercial formulations relied on mixing high-temperature refractory clays with organic matter that completely burns away during the firing stage, leaving behind air pockets.
• US Patent 3591393A (Johns-Manville): This recipe features a fired composition of kaolin clay, pyrophyllite, grog (pre-fired refractory clay), and sawdust, which includes a particulate pre-fired mixture of gypsum and kaolin to improve high-temperature strength.
• US Patent 3309210A: Outlines a strict ratio for organic pore-formers, demonstrating that 10% to 25% wood flour by weight is optimal. Exceeding 25% leaves the fired brick too brittle to withstand shipping and operational handling.

2. High-Strength Bauxite & Silica Blends

For more rigorous industrial applications like boiler linings, recipes shift toward synthetic mineral structures that maintain a low thermal footprint but have increased load-bearing properties.
• Patent CN104909768A: A specific breakdown utilizing 25–35 parts bauxite clinker, 30–40 parts silica powder, 15–20 parts combined clay, and 5–8 parts calcium aluminate cement. The cement ensures rapid cold-set hardening before the brick undergoes kiln-firing.

3. Advanced Chemical Foaming & Synthetic Aggregates

Modern or specialized patents utilize chemical reactions or lightweight polymer beads rather than wood or sawdust to engineer uniform micro-porosity.
• European Patent EP0015135A1: This process details manufacturing bricks from a baseline mixture of refractory aggregate and an inorganic binder, adding granular foamed polystyrene as the pore former. It incorporates 1 to 3 parts by weight of alumina cement (8% to 75% alumina content) and 0.1 to 0.3 parts by weight of water-soluble styrene maleic resin per 100 parts to prevent shrinkage and speed up de-molding.
• US Patent 2242434 (Babcock & Wilcox): Focuses on producing highly permeable, cellular ceramic structures intended for both high-temperature thermal insulation and acoustic dampening.
