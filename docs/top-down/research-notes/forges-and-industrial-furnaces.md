# Roof- and top-fired gas appliances outside pottery: forges, glass, melting, heat-treat and industrial furnaces

Scope: whole appliances whose gas burners sit in the roof, lid or crown and fire downward, tangentially or flat along the roof. Burner hardware (ribbon and ring burners) and pottery kilns are covered by other researchers.

How the sources were read:
- IForgeIron and BladeForums returned HTTP 403 to direct fetches. Every forge-forum claim below comes from search-engine summaries of those threads, not from reading the full thread. The quotes are probably accurate, but the attribution to a particular thread or post can't be pinned down. These claims are marked "(snippet)".
- Patents and vendor pages that were fetched in full are marked "(fetched)".
- Most photo pages (forum threads, maker product pages) could not be opened, so photo descriptions are given only where a page was actually read.

## 1. Blacksmith and knife forges with top-mounted burners, and why builders mount the burner tangentially

### Takeaway
Many commercial forges, such as Majestic, fire straight down from top dead center (TDC). On the forums the stated reason is mostly history: "that's the way most of the first gas forges came out." A straight-down flame hits the floor and splits into two counter-rotating vortices, one left-handed and one right-handed. It also leaves a hot spot and puts flame on the stock. Builders of round forges angle the burner instead (about 2 o'clock, or tangentially at the near edge of the floor) to get one vortex, which they say gives more even heat and longer refractory life. The split into two vortices is a close match for the user's two half-moon arcs. On a forge it is treated as a side effect to avoid.

### Cited Findings

**Confirmed examples (maker, retailer or reviewer pages, or consistent forum testimony):**
- **Majestic Forge (Knifemaker series, 2-, 3- and 5-burner):** forum users describe the burners as pointing straight down from the top. One says "having the jets shoot straight down onto your work isn't very good if you're into knife making" and that it creates "a serious hot spot". Another says Majestic forges "seem to heat the steel up very quickly because the burners point straight down and heat the steel directly." A 2013 poster asked whether running the forge on its side would avoid the straight-down tubes (snippet) — [BladeForums: experience with a Majestic forge](https://bladeforums.com/threads/does-anyone-have-or-have-experience-with-a-majestic-forge.777023); [IForgeIron: Majestic knifemaker forge used on its side](https://www.iforgeiron.com/topic/36187-majestic-knifemaker-forge-used-on-it39s-side/); [IForgeIron: Majestic forge](https://www.iforgeiron.com/topic/37075-majestic-forge/)
- Majestic's own product pages describe an independent needle valve and orifice for each burner (2-burner Deluxe), an independent control valve for each of three burners (3-burner Deluxe), and a long interior for long work (5-burner Deluxe). They say nothing about burner angle. The 2-burner page returned 404 when fetched, so the photos were not seen (snippet) — [Majestic 5-burner Knifemaker Deluxe](https://www.majesticforge.com/Majestic-5-Burner-Knifemaker-Deluxe.html); [Majestic 3-burner Knifemaker Economy](https://www.majesticforge.com/3-Burner-Knifemaker-Economy.html). Those pages should show the burner tubes standing vertically out of the roof of a rectangular box forge, but I could not open them to confirm.
- **Diamondback Ironworks:** the "economy" model is described as having "the burners entering at an angle, and the mouth of the forge is wider." Another user liked the angled burner but said the short distance to the work still makes "a direct-impact hot spot" (snippet) — [BladeForums: experience with a Majestic forge](https://bladeforums.com/threads/does-anyone-have-or-have-experience-with-a-majestic-forge.777023). The company owner is quoted as saying every forge in the line reaches welding heat, that gas pressure rather than burner count sets the temperature, and that "our single burners get as hot as our 3 burner models" (snippet) — [IForgeIron: Diamondback forges](https://www.iforgeiron.com/topic/13899-dimondback-forges/); [IForgeIron: Diamondback forge review](https://www.iforgeiron.com/topic/54504-diamondback-forge-review/)
- **Atlas Knife and Tool forges:** a knifedogs poster says the burners "come in from the side", a choice made by the maker, a knifemaker (snippet, unverified against Atlas documents) — [Knifedogs: forge thread](https://www.knifedogs.com/threads/forge.53175). A retailer lists a "swirling chamber", a 2.5 in diameter × 11 in chamber and a maximum of 2500 °F — [Pops Knife Supply: Atlas forge](https://popsknife.supplies/products/atlas-forge). So Atlas appears to be a swirl forge, but its burner is not in the roof.
- **Mr. Volcano (2-burner kit):** the steel shell comes with the burner ports already welded on, so the builder can't change their position. The chamber is an open-ended pentagonal tube lined with ceramic-fibre blanket that has pre-cut holes for the burner nozzles, coated with rigidizer and two coats of Satanite. Exhaust leaves through the open ends; after curing there was carbon "only on the outer lip at the top". Problems the reviewer reported: slow drying, cement that cracked easily, and grit from the cement in the burner threads. Photos in the review show the boxed kit, the salmon-pink blanket with the burner nozzles poking through, the cement-coated interior with a cracked spot near one end, and the forge firing on one burner on wooden rounds (fetched) — [Doc Hammer: review of Mr. Volcano 2-burner forge](https://dochammer.substack.com/p/review-mr-volcano-2-burner-forge). The review does not say whether the burners point straight down or tangentially.
- **NC Tool (Whisper Baby, Momma, Lowboy):** the Whisper Baby has one burner, a solid front door and 1.5 × 3 in ports at each end. The Momma has two burners and a rear door; the Lowboy has three burners. A forum user reports that on the Whisper Baby the burner lines up with the rear stock door, so hot exhaust is pulled out through that door when it is open; the suggested fixes are turning the burner 90° or adding a sheet-metal guard. Crud builds up "right at the flame holder/bottom end of the burner" (snippet) — [American Farriers: NC Whisper Baby](https://www.americanfarriers.com/articles/12195-nc-tool-co-inc-nc-whisper-baby-gas-forge); [IForgeIron: tuning an NC Tool forge](https://www.iforgeiron.com/topic/26524-tuning-an-nc-tool-forge/); [IForgeIron: NC Tool forge question](https://www.iforgeiron.com/topic/26140-nc-tool-forge-question/)
- **Gas horseshoe forge patents:** "a specially designed burner connected to the top of the housing" of a farrier field forge (snippet; patents not fetched) — [US4545361A](https://patents.google.com/patent/US4545361); [US4576574A](https://patents.google.com/patent/US4576574)

**Why TDC, and why builders go tangential (forum consensus, snippet):**
- Top mounting is largely historical: "That's the way most of the first gas forges came out", and makers kept it. One poster contrasts DIY plans, which put the burner "at an angle at around 2 o'clock", with "professional forges for sale [where] the burners are mounted exactly vertical" — [IForgeIron: Forges 101, p.19](https://www.iforgeiron.com/topic/47439-forges-101/page/19/); [IForgeIron: burner placement](https://www.iforgeiron.com/topic/61646-burner-placement/); [IForgeIron: burner angle in a cylindrical forge](https://www.iforgeiron.com/topic/14128-burner-angle-in-a-cylindrical-forge/). The quotes appeared across these threads, and I could not tell which one each came from.
- **A straight-down TDC burner splits the flame "into two swirls of left and right hand spirals".** The forge in question could only be run with the burner turned low, because turning it up would make the flame "impinge on the stock, causing a lot of trouble" (snippet) — same threads as above, plus [IForgeIron: school me on burner angle](https://www.iforgeiron.com/topic/54578-school-me-on-burner-angle-please/)
- "Top positioned and facing straight down burners bisect the work area; exposing your parts to flame damage." A shallow angle aimed at the near side of the floor keeps the flame off the parts and still makes a swirl (snippet) — [IForgeIron: burner placement](https://www.iforgeiron.com/topic/61646-burner-placement/)
- Tangential or angled entry, with the flame hitting "the floor at an angle to induce a strong vortex", is said to give much more even temperature and longer refractory life. Vertically mounted burners mix "more chaotically" (snippet) — [IForgeIron: burner angle in a cylindrical forge](https://www.iforgeiron.com/topic/14128-burner-angle-in-a-cylindrical-forge/); [BladeForums: burner tangents](https://www.bladeforums.com/threads/burner-tangents.628532/)
- Common aiming advice: the flame should land near the near edge of the shelf or floor, clear of the wall (snippet) — [IForgeIron: burner angle, am I understanding this correctly](https://www.iforgeiron.com/topic/67286-burner-angle-am-i-understanding-this-correctly/)
- Burner ports are sometimes ground slightly elliptical so the burner can be aimed on a tangent, which keeps the flame landing near the edge of the floor (snippet) — [IForgeIron: burner location](https://www.iforgeiron.com/topic/32632-burner-location/)
- Side or bottom mounting is said to keep the flame in the chamber longer ("hangtime") so more of the fuel burns inside (snippet) — [IForgeIron: first forge build plans](https://www.iforgeiron.com/topic/60917-first-forge-build-plans/)
- Problems specific to top mounting: "burners inserted vertically have more of a chimney effect issue when you shut down the forge" (hot gas rises up the burner tube), and "the gas hose gets a lot more hot than I am comfortable with" (snippet) — [IForgeIron: is sideways burner orientation effective](https://www.iforgeiron.com/topic/77481-burner-orientation-is-sideways-effective/); [IForgeIron: concept horizontal front mount burner](https://www.iforgeiron.com/topic/67604-concept-horizontal-front-mount-burner/)
- For a flat (rectangular) forge, one commenter says several smaller burners across the top spread the heat over a larger area. He warns that burners spaced too closely can cause pressure problems (snippet) — [IForgeIron: first forge build plans](https://www.iforgeiron.com/topic/60917-first-forge-build-plans/)
- A farrier-forge thread says top-mounted, downward-facing burners are not even desirable, and offers side-mounted, upward-facing burners as an alternative (snippet) — [IForgeIron: farrier forge burners](https://www.iforgeiron.com/topic/64218-farrier-forge-burners/)
- "A square chambered forge with burners pointed straight at the middle will have issues with hotspots on your work" (snippet) — [Knifedogs: forge burner location](https://knifedogs.com/threads/forge-burner-location.42469/latest)

**DIY ribbon-burner forges mounted in the roof (snippet):**
- A builder who mounted a ribbon burner "from the top blowing strait down" saw that "the flame came down to the floor then divided and had two swirling vortexes", the same split seen with venturi burners — [IForgeIron: optimal ribbon burner angle on a D-shape forge](https://www.iforgeiron.com/topic/79529-optimal-ribbon-burner-angle-on-d-shape-forge/); [IForgeIron: a slightly different forge design](https://www.iforgeiron.com/topic/67055-a-slightly-different-forge-design/) (the quote appeared across these threads)
- "With ribbon burners, positioning is less critical than with any other kind", because "combustion is complete at the burner and not dependent on the swirling motion" (snippet) — [IForgeIron: ribbon burner](https://www.iforgeiron.com/topic/39568-ribbon-burner/)
- Reported hazards: "a large VERY LOUD explosion in the forge after 45 minutes to an hour of use", which one poster blamed on the nozzle passages ("nozlettes") heating up. Kast-O-Lite 30 is the refractory usually used for the burner block (snippet) — [IForgeIron: ribbon burner overheat](https://www.iforgeiron.com/topic/66730-ribbon-burner-overheat/); [IForgeIron: naturally aspirated ribbon burner](https://www.iforgeiron.com/topic/48001-naturally-aspirated-ribbon-burner-photo-heavy/). The thread titles say "photo heavy" and "post pics of your ribbon burner forge", but I could not open them to see the photos — [IForgeIron: post pics of your ribbon burner forge](https://www.iforgeiron.com/topic/40463-post-pics-of-your-ribbon-burner-forge-where-your-gas-inlet-feed-into-the-burner/)

**Temperature and size figures from retail listings (low reliability):**
- Hell's Forge double-burner: "over 2300°F", 19 × 6.75 × 3.5 in workspace. Single burner: over 2300 °F, 23 lb. These come from third-party retailer listings that contain garbled data, and they don't say where the burner sits — [desertcart: Hell's Forge](https://www.desertcart.com.cy/products/67690539)
- Anvilfire P-75 burner instructions: 3–25 psi; fires roughly 300–500 cubic inches of forge at full temperature; forges "must ALWAYS have a vent" — [Anvilfire P-75 burner instructions (PDF)](https://www.anvilfire.com/prod-docs/p75-burner-inst.pdf)

**Unconfirmed:**
- **Chile Forge:** no source states how Chile Forge mounts its burners. One knifemaking-forum poster says Chile Forge is the commercial forge he would come closest to recommending, though "they still have some issues". Another says the company used a burner design without a licence. These are claims about reputation, not layout (snippet) — [Knifedogs: whats going on here, p.2](https://knifedogs.com/threads/ok-gang-whats-going-on-here.45268/page-2); [IForgeIron: new Chile forge](https://www.iforgeiron.com/topic/30420-new-chile-forge/)
- **Hell's Forge** burner position: not confirmed.

### Inferences
- The two counter-rotating vortices forge builders see under a TDC burner come from one downward jet hitting the floor and splitting into two horizontal arcs. That is a crude version of the user's "two half-moon arcs". Forge builders treat it as something to avoid because of what comes with it: a hot stripe straight under the burner where the jet lands, and flame on the work. A manifold that forms the two arcs at the lid, rather than relying on the floor to split them, would avoid that hot stripe.
- In forge practice, "even" means one coherent vortex. Every recommendation for evenness is single-direction swirl from an off-axis burner. No source recommends opposed or split swirl for evenness in a small round chamber.
- Top-mounted burners also bring a chimney effect up the burner tube at shutdown and a hot gas hose above the appliance. Both matter for a lid manifold.

### Gaps
- Chile Forge, Hell's Forge and Diamondback burner angles: no maker documentation found. I could not get into the forum threads to check photos.
- No measured temperature uniformity (thermocouple surveys) for any forge, TDC or tangential. All the claims about evenness are anecdotal.
- No photo was seen first-hand on any forge maker's page. The URLs above are where the photos would be.

## 2. Glass: pot furnaces, day tanks, glory holes and annealers with burners in the lid or crown

### Takeaway
I found one documented precedent for a top-fired glory hole: an 1893 patent with the burner above a slot in the roof, a spreader that turns the flame into a downward sheet, and exhaust through arches at the bottom of the side walls. In industry, crown burners (mostly oxy-fuel) are a recognised but specialised option that puts heavy demands on the crown refractory. I found no published burner layout for the studio makers named in the brief (Hub, Correll, Spiral Arts, Wet Dog). One university SOP says gas glory holes usually have the burner at a right angle to the opening, not in the roof.

### Cited Findings

**Confirmed:**
- **US 509,146 "Glory-hole furnace" (C. D. Trimble, granted 21 Nov 1893):** the burner sits above an elongated slot in the roof plate and fires down into a rectangular chamber. A tubular cast "flame spreader" has a flared lower end that rests on the roof plate; sharp inward-projecting edges inside it reduce the pressure in the middle of the jet and spread the flame into a vertical sheet. The patent says a shaped burner mouth can do the same job. The sheet strikes the ends of the bottle necks at right angles. **Exhaust leaves through "smoke arches" in the lower edges of the side walls**, one below each glory hole (or through a continuous slot under the walls), so the furnace is top-fired with bottom exhaust. The fuel was oil with compressed air. Stated benefits: necks heat evenly without overheating the shoulders, and less discoloration than a grate furnace. The patent figures show the chamber, spreader and stand (fetched) — [Google Patents US509146](https://patents.google.com/patent/US509146)
- **Studio glory-hole practice:** the West Texas A&M hot-shop SOP says gas-fired glory holes "most commonly" place the burner at a right angle to the working opening, and that the opening needs an aperture door system so its size can be changed (snippet) — [WTAMU Hot Shop Glass Furnace and Kiln Safety SOP (PDF)](https://wtamu.edu/webres/File/Academics/Graduate%20School/AR-EHS/SOPs/24_01_99_W1_02AT%20WTAMU%20Hot%20Shop%20Glass%20Furnance%20and%20Kiln%20Safety.pdf)
- **Industrial crown (roof) burners:**
  - US 6,540,508 covers installing roof-mounted oxy-fuel burners in a glass melting furnace. It says melting had traditionally used electrodes or "flames generated substantially horizontally". The crown is drilled hot or cold, care is taken to avoid thermal shock, and the outside of the crown is prepared for a gas-tight seal around the burner block (snippet) — [Google Patents US6540508](https://patents.google.com/patent/US6540508)
  - US 8,578,738: a method of melting a batch with a crown burner (snippet; not read in detail) — [Google Patents US8578738](https://patents.google.com/patent/US8578738)
  - Linde sells vertical burners for the crown of glass melting furnaces and claims roughly twice the heating power of conventional wall burners (vendor claim, snippet) — [Linde: burners for the glass industry](https://www.linde-gas.nl/shop/en/nl-ig/burners-for-the-glass-industry)
  - US 10,273,178 puts side-wall burners at 35–65% of wall height, angled up to 15° toward the crown. Its stated aims include reducing NOx and "deterioration of the interior surface of the roof", which confirms crown damage is a known concern (snippet) — [Google Patents US10273178](https://patents.google.com/patent/US10273178)
  - CA 2,202,427 raises oxy-fuel ports to about 18–36 in above the glass to reduce alkali corrosion (snippet) — [CIPO CA2202427](https://brevets-patents.ic.gc.ca/opic-cipo/cpd/eng/patent/2202427/summary.html)
  - A LinkedIn explainer (low reliability) describes top-fired furnaces with downward flames, says they suit glass with tight temperature limits (ultra-clear, electronic), and says the crown must withstand heavy thermal load and cycling (snippet) — [LinkedIn: top-fired vs side-fired glass furnaces](https://eg.linkedin.com/in/khaledsalamasuez/en)
- **Charles Correll:** designed and built his first recuperative glass furnace in 1980 and adapted industrial methods for small studios. The online teaser does not describe burner placement; the full article is in *Glass Art*, Sept/Oct 2014, p.54 (fetched). The only photo is a thumbnail of Correll — [Glass Art: Pioneers in Glass, Charles Correll](https://glassartmagazine.com/articles/general-interest/item/1566-pioneers-in-glass-charles-correll-king-of-recuperation)
- **Spiral Arts:** the only related document found is an electric glory-hole element baffle patent, assigned to Spiral Arts and the Corning Museum of Glass, which is not gas (snippet) — [Google Patents US10059619](https://patents.google.com/patent/US10059619)

**Unconfirmed:**
- Hub Consolidated, Wet Dog Glass and Spiral Arts gas furnace, glory hole and annealer burner positions: no source found.
- DIY studio furnaces fired down through the crown: no source found.

### Inferences
- The 1893 glory hole is the closest small-appliance analogue to the user's lid burner. It fires down through the roof, spreads the flame mechanically into a sheet, and draws exhaust out low. That makes it a downdraft layout, which is what a lid-fired kiln with a low flue would be.
- In industrial glass practice, roof firing is chosen to put heat straight onto a horizontal melt surface. The price is crown penetration and the work of sealing and maintaining it.

### Gaps
- No studio-scale (Hub, Correll, Wet Dog, Spiral Arts) layouts. The Correll article is behind a print issue. Glass-forum threads did not show up in search.
- No data on uniformity or temperature for crown-fired studio equipment.

## 3. Metal-casting melting furnaces (crucible and ladle) with the burner in the lid

### Takeaway
Standard crucible furnaces fire tangentially at the bottom. A tangential inlet and outlet turning the same way is what gives an orderly swirl, and a flame aimed straight at the crucible is avoided because it causes hot spots and cracks crucibles. Lid burners do exist. One is a cast-iron hybrid induction furnace with three burners in a pivoting lid. More widely, **steel-mill ladle preheaters** are lid-fired: a burner sits in a refractory cover, fired either straight down the axis or with several burners around the cover's edge angled tangentially. The gas feed to the moving cover runs through a steel flexible hose. Burner life on a hot lid is a stated concern.

### Cited Findings

**Confirmed:**
- **US 10,598,437 "Hybrid metal melting furnace" (cast iron and similar):** three burners are mounted in the first lid, arranged around its central axis, and fire into the crucible through holes in the lid, alongside an induction coil. A C-shaped ring hood around the opening pulls gas and fume into a duct; the lid does not seal and the pour spout stays open. **There are two lids because a burner lid left over molten metal while holding would expose the burners to heat for long periods and "shorten the service life of the burner".** A second lid without burners is swung in for holding. No temperatures are given (fetched) — [Google Patents US10598437](https://patents.google.com/patent/US10598437)
- **US 4,014,532 "Ladle refractory lining preheater" (C. A. Holley, filed 1976, granted 29 Mar 1977):** an oil or gas burner "projects centrally and axially" into a concave refractory-lined ladle cover laid on the ladle mouth. In the variant of FIGS. 5–6, **several burners around the cover's periphery are angled "substantially tangentially" to spread heat more evenly**. One or two exhaust stacks on the cover connect through flexible tubes to the plant flue; FIG. 5 uses a central flue. Gas reaches the burner through a needle valve, a "steel flexible hose", a pressure gauge and a high-pressure inspirator, so the burner can move with the boom. The cover hangs from a boom on a hydraulic or air cylinder, with a pivot that keeps the cover level. Prior-art problem: covers held away from the ladle mouth to vent let fumes and spatter reach workers (fetched) — [Google Patents US4014532A](https://patents.google.com/patent/US4014532A/en)
- A 1998 conference paper on a lid-mounted burner for electric-furnace ladles gives a preheat temperature of about 1270 K and says the exhaust is too hot for metallic recuperators. The solution was a regenerative burner (NKK / Nippon Furnace Kogyo) with ceramic honeycomb regenerators, two burners alternating every 30 s (snippet; the record ID within the collection was not confirmed) — [University of Utah collection, item 13374](https://collections.lib.utah.edu/details?id=13374); [item 13384](https://collections.lib.utah.edu/details?id=13384)
- US 4,718,643 covers rapid high-temperature ladle preheating (not read) — [USPTO PDF 4718643](https://image-ppubs.uspto.gov/dirsearch-public/print/downloadPdf/4718643)
- **The standard tangential crucible furnace, for contrast:**
  - A German crucible-furnace patent says the inlet and outlet channels should both run tangentially in the same rotational direction but at different heights. Making only the inlet tangential gave poorer results; the tangential outlet mattered for an "orderly" swirl (snippet, translated) — [Google Patents DEP0001754BA](https://patents.google.com/patent/DEP0001754BA/en)
  - US 4,455,016: crucibles heated from the outside develop temperature gradients that fracture them, "typically after only 3 to 6 months"; crucible furnace efficiency is about 7–17%. The flame is directed tangentially to circulate in the annular space (snippet) — [USPTO PDF 4455016](https://image-ppubs.uspto.gov/dirsearch-public/print/downloadPdf/4455016)
  - An older oil-fired patent: "the burning heating gases ... are conducted tangentially into the combustion chamber where they circulate about the crucible"; the lid is only a cover (snippet) — [USPTO PDF 4378105](https://image-ppubs.uspto.gov/dirsearch-public/print/downloadPdf/4378105)
  - A hobby engineering page advises driving hot gas around the crucible rather than straight at it, to avoid hot spots that crack it. Its figure of "cracking in 20–30 heats" is unverified (snippet) — [Firgelli: gas crucible furnace mechanism](https://www.firgelliauto.com/blogs/mechanisms/gas-crucible-furnace)

**Unconfirmed:**
- No hobby or commercial aluminium or bronze crucible furnace with a burner in the lid was found. Searches on r/Metalcasting and alloyavenue terms returned nothing relevant.

### Inferences
- The tangential-periphery ladle cover (US 4,014,532, FIGS. 5–6) is the closest industrial precedent to the user's idea: a removable lid, several burners around its edge, tangential firing into a vessel, a flue on the cover, and a flexible steel hose to the moving cover.
- The German patent's point that both inlet and outlet must be tangential suggests that where the flue sits will decide whether a lid-generated swirl lasts. A central flue in the lid, or one low on the axis, would compete with the swirl.

### Gaps
- No temperatures, firing rates or uniformity figures for lid-fired crucible or ladle units, apart from the snippet giving about 1270 K ladle preheat.
- No hobby lid-fired crucible furnace builds found.

## 4. Heat-treat (bell, top-hat, car-bottom) and industrial roof-fired furnaces: how they get even heat and where the flue goes

### Takeaway
Heat-treat bell, top-hat and car-bottom furnaces are mostly **side-fired**, with burners above and below the load. Their uniformity comes from high-velocity jets, circulation and pulse firing, not from burners in the roof. True roof firing is mainstream in two big industrial families. **Steel reheat furnaces with flat-flame or radiant roof burners** spread the flame thin along the roof so the roof radiates onto the charge, use clockwise and counter-clockwise burner pairs for an even heat flux, and claim more uniform charge temperatures. **Top-fired steam-methane reformers** fire burner rows down between tube rows and exhaust through floor tunnels under each burner row, a downdraft. Their known failures are flames bending onto the tubes and flue gas spreading unevenly along the tunnels.

### Cited Findings

**Flat-flame and radiant-roof burners (confirmed):**
- **Tenova TR flat-flame roof burners:** described as "radiant burners" for radiant roofs. **Two types sit on one roof, one with a clockwise-rotating flame and one counter-clockwise, which "ensures a highly uniform heat flux distribution over the charge."** Flame shape is tuned with CFD for high turbulence. Combustion air preheat up to 550 °C; NOx test conditions of furnace ≤1250 °C and air ≤450 °C; expected NOx 280 mg/Nm³ on natural gas at 3% O₂; CO < 5 ppm; turndown 10–100%. Related products listed are walking-beam, walking-hearth, pusher and rotary-hearth furnaces. The page has two photos and a 3D render labelled "TR burner", plus a drawing and capacity and dimension charts whose content I could not read. The flue location is not stated (fetched) — [Tenova: TR flat flame roof burners](https://tenova.com/technologies/tr-flat-flame-roof-burners)
- **ispatguru, burners in reheating furnaces:** flat-flame burners (FFBs) are "usually used on the roof", where they "spread the flame over the roof surface in a thin layer". Axial flow is negligible, so the roof becomes "the chief radiating surface". These are called indirect-heating furnaces because the flame does not touch the charge. "The temperature distribution in the FFB furnace chamber and in the steel charge material is more uniform." They suit charges with a large flat surface heated above 900 °C, and fuel use can drop by up to almost 40% when output falls from 100% to 50%. **Drawbacks:** a sudden change in output can overheat the charge surface; flame on the charge increases scale; incomplete-combustion products attack the refractory; obstructions cause black smoke; "the flame must not hit the roof" (fetched) — [ispatguru: types of burners in reheating furnaces](https://www.ispatguru.com/types-of-burners-in-reheating-furnaces/)
- **Fives North American catalogue (flat-flame section):** the flame heats the surrounding refractory, and "the refractory then radiates heat uniformly to the load". The flat flame limits "the possibility of hot spots caused by flame impingement on work as close as 12 inches away". Roof-mounted burners are said to heat wire patenting furnaces more evenly than side firing (snippet; the page I fetched, 537, was a radiant-tube page, so these claims were not checked on the page itself) — [Fives North American combustion catalogue p.530](https://online.flippingbook.com/view/139515/530/)
- US 5,813,846 (low-NOx flat-flame burner): a secondary injector widens the flame, which lowers "heat flux per unit of wall/roof surface area" and gives "more uniform heating across the wall and roof" (snippet) — [USPTO PDF 5813846](https://image-ppubs.uspto.gov/dirsearch-public/print/downloadPdf/5813846)
- US 2015/0176098 (furnace for heating metal goods): roof burners fire the upper furnace when the furnace is wider than a certain value, because they give uniform temperature across the width, "however, a uniform temperature distribution cannot be adjusted in the lower furnace by means of these roof burners" (snippet) — [Justia 20150176098](https://patents.justia.com/patent/20150176098)
- US 4,045,160: a flat-flame gas burner that uses swirl to make a broad flat flame, aimed at direct-fired steel-strip annealing furnaces (snippet) — [Justia 4045160](https://patents.justia.com/patent/4045160)

**Top-fired steam-methane reformers (confirmed, layout well documented):**
- Burners sit in rows in the roof, between rows of catalyst tubes and between the tubes and the walls, and fire downward. **Flue gas flows down and leaves through openings in refractory exhaust tunnels on the floor, one tunnel directly under each burner row**, with the tube rows between the tunnels — [USPTO PDF 11110424](https://image-ppubs.uspto.gov/dirsearch-public/print/downloadPdf/11110424); [Google Patents EP3182003A1](https://patents.google.com/patent/EP3182003)
- A known uniformity problem: more flue gas enters the tunnels near their open end than near the closed end, so the tubes heat unevenly (snippet) — same patent family, [Justia 10711996](https://patents.justia.com/patent/10711996)
- One industrial top-fired reformer used in a CFD study had 64 tubes, 30 burners and 3 flue-gas tunnels (snippet) — [arXiv 2501.08660](https://export.arxiv.org/pdf/2501.08660)
- Johnson Matthey: flue gas in a top-fired reformer flows downward to collection tunnels at the floor and on to the convection section and an induced-draft fan (snippet) — [Johnson Matthey: low-rate SMR operation Q&A (PDF)](https://matthey.com/documents/161599/162780/Low-Rate-SMR-Operation-QA.pdf/8a6b8ba3-bf89-3630-71e4-9337aaa09724)
- **Failure mode:** in down-fired designs, weak flame momentum lets flames bend back onto the tubes, coking the inside wall and overheating the fired side (snippet) — [USPTO PDF 10088154: down-fired burner with a perforated flame holder](https://image-ppubs.uspto.gov/dirsearch-public/print/downloadPdf/10088154). Flames touching the refractory wear it locally and show up as hot spots on the shell (snippet) — [BIC Magazine: managing hot spots in primary reformers](https://www.bicmagazine.com/resources/sponsored-content/managing-hot-spots-in-primary-reformers/). John Zink lists visual signs of flame impingement and fixes such as adjusting burners and air registers and cleaning gas tips (snippet) — [John Zink: flame impingement on process tubes](https://www.johnzink.com/resources-training/knowledge-hub/understanding-and-preventing-flame-impingement-on-process-tubes)

**Heat-treat bell, top-hat and car-bottom furnaces (counter-examples: side-fired):**
- **US 4,142,712 (multistand batch coil annealing):** the burners are in the **side walls**, about 3 ft 8 in above the base. High-momentum jets (about 3 in nozzle, about 15° cone) fire into the gaps between inner covers. Burners on opposite sides fire into alternating gaps, so the gas circulates around each cover in the opposite direction to its neighbours ("serpentine" flow). **The flue is low, just above each burner, behind a radiation shield**, with air-preheat tubes in it. Annealing range about 1100–1700 °F; acceptable variation across a cover about 200 °F; a scale mock-up ran at 1480 °F with a maximum of 1580 °F near the jet and peak jet velocity 8,250 fpm. Prior art: direct-fired low-velocity burners "require a large number of burners" and have "inherent limitations" on placement (fetched) — [Google Patents US4142712A](https://patents.google.com/patent/US4142712A/en)
- US 3,733,174 (covered heat-treatment furnace): burners low on the body, **exhaust hole in the roof**, and the load heated indirectly through an inner cover (snippet) — [Google Patents US3733174A](https://patents.google.com/patent/US3733174A/esLanguage)
- **Car-bottom furnaces:** NUTEC Bickley uses high-velocity burners "positioned in staggered configuration to fire above and below the load", with 11 control zones (five top, six bottom) and pulse firing (snippet) — [Heat Treat Today: large car bottom furnace](https://www.heattreattoday.com/?p=30214). L&L: six medium-velocity burners firing over and under the load, uniformity expected at ±25 °F / 12.5 °C or better (snippet) — [L&L Special Furnace press release](https://www.llfurnace.com/news/press-release-order-for-gas-fired-car-bottom-furnace-for-heat-treating-steel-pipe-and-fittings). US 4,310,301: high-momentum burners in the side walls "adjacent the top and bottom", plus exhaust recirculation (snippet) — [Google Patents US4310301](https://patents.google.com/patent/US4310301)
- Top-hat furnaces: the body lifts over a fixed base. The listing found is for an electric unit (Deltec), not gas (snippet) — [Equipt: Deltec DT-29 top hat furnace](https://www.equipt.com/listings/291705-used-2012-deltec-dt-29-taa-36-top-hat-furnaces-in-colorado)

### Inferences
- Roof firing gives even heat in industry in one of two ways. Either the flame is spread flat along the roof so the roof becomes a large, even radiator (reheat furnaces, with flat-flame or radiant-cup burners), or the burners fire long downward flames in parallel lanes with exhaust drawn out at the floor directly beneath (reformers). Neither swirls the gas around a perimeter.
- Tenova's mix of clockwise and counter-clockwise burners on one roof, and the serpentine alternating flow in the coil annealer, both rely on alternating the direction of rotation for evenness. That is related to the user's two opposed half-moon arcs.
- Heat-treat furnaces that need even load temperatures (car-bottom, bell) avoid roof burners and use over-and-under side firing with high velocity or pulse firing.

### Gaps
- No flue-location detail for flat-flame roof reheat furnaces. In practice they are continuous furnaces that exhaust at the charge end, but no source here says so.
- No Bloom Engineering, Selas (Duradiant), Maxon, Hauck or Eclipse application notes were reached. The North American Combustion Handbook and NFPA 86 text were not reached either.
- No top-hat or bell heat-treat furnace with burners in the hood roof was found.

## 5. Periodic industrial ceramic kilns (shuttle, top-hat) from Swindell Dressler, Riedhammer, Keith, Harrop and Bickley: any roof firing?

### Takeaway
Periodic kilns are mostly side-fired (car-bottom style, firing above and below the setting). Patents mention ceiling burners only as an option. **Roof firing is well established in continuous brick tunnel kilns ("top firing").** Rows of roof injectors or high-velocity burners fire down into the gaps between stacks. Swindell Dressler patented a low-profile brick kiln with about 72 high-velocity burners all in the roof (peak about 1125 °C, exhaust drawn back through the preheat zone). The known drawback of downward roof firing is that the top of the setting heats faster than the bottom. A Fives North American patent addresses it by switching between "spin" air for a short flame and "forward" air for a long one.

### Cited Findings

**Confirmed (continuous kilns):**
- **US 5,667,378 "Low profile kiln apparatus" (Swindell Dressler International, inventor James Bushman; filed 1992, granted 16 Sep 1997):** a tunnel kiln for bricks set no more than two high (load ≤18 in). **About 72 high-velocity gas burners, all in the roof, none below the car.** In the early furnace zone there are six rows of six burners in the flat roof, firing at an angle toward the load and against the direction of travel. In the later zone there are three raised roof sections, each with two rows of six burners firing at each other across the kiln width. Swindell Dressler "Dyna-Max" control uses motorized air valves to make an oscillating combustion front that sweeps the load, which "reduces hot spotting compared with static firing". Each burner row is one zone with a crown thermocouple. Peak firing zone about 1125 °C. **Exhaust:** drawn back through the preheat zone into wall offtakes above and below load level, then to an exhaust fan and a stack. Stated prior-art problems: conventional kilns need burners above and below the load, firing takes 30–80 h, and static firing causes hot spots (fetched) — [Google Patents US5667378A](https://patents.google.com/patent/US5667378A/en)
- **US 7,637,739 / US 8,087,930 (Fives North American Combustion):** a roof-fired tunnel kiln for bricks and tiles. Injectors in rows across the roof are "aimed vertically downward toward the spaces between the stacks". **Problem: projecting flames downward "causes the stacks of bricks to become heated more quickly near the top than the bottom."** Fix: inject fuel and oxidant separately so they auto-ignite beside the load; "spin air" gives a short flame and "forward air" a longer one, and changing the split moves the hottest band up or down the stack while total heat input stays the same. Other burners first bring the zone up to auto-ignition temperature. No temperatures, uniformity numbers or flue path are given (fetched) — [Google Patents US7637739B2](https://patents.google.com/patent/US7637739B2/en); [Justia 8087930](https://patents.justia.com/patent/8087930)
- US 6,572,369 (tunnel kiln and burner): the background says many kilns use "a plurality of small burners which are typically secured in the roof", each with small output, and that their firing efficiency "is not satisfactory". The invention hangs tubular burners vertically from the ceiling, with nozzles between the brick faces so gas flows along the perforations. The assignee was not confirmed (snippet) — [Google Patents US6572369](https://patents.google.com/patent/US6572369)
- US 2,003,451 (kiln): roof injectors made of paired nozzles with valve plates that partly or fully cover the openings (snippet) — [Google Patents US2003451A](https://patents.google.com/patent/US2003451A/en)
- **Hoffmann (ring) kilns:** the roof had rows of "feed holes" with airtight caps. Stokers dropped crushed coal through them onto brick "fireplaces" built into the setting below each hole. "Some Hoffmann kilns were fuelled by gas, but mostly the fuel was coal" (snippet). This is a historical precedent for top-feeding fuel; whether gas was fed through the same holes was not confirmed — [Low-tech Magazine: Rings of fire, Hoffmann kilns](https://solar.lowtechmagazine.com/2009/10/rings-of-fire-hoffmann-kilns/)

**Periodic (shuttle and top-hat) kilns:**
- A recent shuttle-kiln patent describes side-wall burners and says some burners may "alternatively pass through the ceiling". Flue risers rise from the floor into flue channels under it (snippet) — [USPTO PDF 12392556: shuttle kiln exhaust configuration](https://image-ppubs.uspto.gov/dirsearch-public/print/downloadPdf/12392556)
- Older updraft shuttle kilns put burners low and exhausted at the top, so the gas passed the ware once, which wasted fuel (snippet) — [Google Patents US3854865A](https://patents.google.com/patent/US3854865A/en). A later design fires side-wall burners upward, turns the gas down through the ware and recirculates it (snippet) — [Justia 4069010](https://patents.justia.com/patent/4069010)
- A kiln-car patent describes periodic kilns with both upper and lower burners (snippet) — [USPTO PDF 10712093](https://image-ppubs.uspto.gov/dirsearch-public/print/downloadPdf/10712093)
- A BHEL tender for a 1700 °C top-hat or bell kiln specifies dual-fuel high-velocity burners with pilots and about 24–26 h total heating and cooling. Burner position was not given (snippet) — [BHEL NIT 3013 (PDF)](https://tenders.bhel.com/sites/default/files/NIT_3013_1.pdf)

**Unconfirmed:**
- Riedhammer: its patents found concern fine-ceramic tunnel kilns with side-wall jets. No roof-fired Riedhammer periodic kiln was found (snippet) — [Justia: patents assigned to Ludwig Riedhammer GmbH](https://patents.justia.com/assignee/ludwig-riedhammer-gmbh)
- Keith Company, Harrop, Bickley: no roof-fired periodic kiln was found. The Bickley car-bottom heat-treat furnace in section 4 fires above and below the load from the side.

### Inferences
- Roof firing in ceramics is a continuous-kiln (brick tunnel) technique. Low, open settings with gaps between stacks let downward jets reach in, and exhaust is pulled out sideways through wall offtakes or back along the kiln. For a periodic kiln with a lid, the "top heats faster than bottom" problem in US 7,637,739 is the most directly relevant warning. Its fix, varying flame length, is also relevant.

### Gaps
- No catalogue or brochure from Swindell Dressler, Riedhammer, Keith, Harrop or Bickley describing a roof-fired shuttle or top-hat kiln. No flue sizing or uniformity numbers for roof-fired brick kilns.

## 6. Reported failure modes: roof refractory damage, flame on the work, overheating burner tiles, flashback, lid warping, gas lines on moving lids

### Takeaway
The failures reported across these appliances are consistent. Downward flames hit the work and leave hot spots (forges, reformers). The top of a load heats faster than the bottom (roof-fired brick kilns). Products of incomplete combustion attack the refractory, and a roof can be damaged if the flame touches it (reheat furnaces). Burner tiles crack from thermal cycling, and hot gas leaks round the tile's expansion gap and overheats the mounting plate. On a hot lid, burner life suffers, which is why one design swaps in a second lid without burners. Top-mounted forge burners send heat up the burner tube at shutdown and make the gas hose hot. Ribbon burners in forges have backfired explosively. Industry handles moving lids with steel flexible hose and boom-mounted controls. NFPA 86 rules on this were not reached.

### Cited Findings
- **Flame on the work and hot spots:** a straight-down TDC forge flame "bisect[s] the work area"; it has to be turned low or it "impinges on the stock" (snippet) — [IForgeIron: burner placement](https://www.iforgeiron.com/topic/61646-burner-placement/); [IForgeIron: Forges 101 p.19](https://www.iforgeiron.com/topic/47439-forges-101/page/19/). Majestic straight-down burners make "a serious hot spot" (snippet) — [BladeForums thread 777023](https://bladeforums.com/threads/does-anyone-have-or-have-experience-with-a-majestic-forge.777023)
- **Down-fired flames bending onto tubes and coking them** in reformers (snippet) — [USPTO PDF 10088154](https://image-ppubs.uspto.gov/dirsearch-public/print/downloadPdf/10088154); unchecked impingement progresses from carbon to scale, bulging and pinhole leaks (snippet) — [John Zink](https://www.johnzink.com/resources-training/knowledge-hub/understanding-and-preventing-flame-impingement-on-process-tubes)
- **Uneven heating top to bottom:** downward roof flames heat brick stacks "more quickly near the top than the bottom" (fetched) — [Google Patents US7637739B2](https://patents.google.com/patent/US7637739B2/en). Roof burners cannot even out the lower furnace (snippet) — [Justia 20150176098](https://patents.justia.com/patent/20150176098)
- **Roof refractory damage:** with flat-flame roof burners, "the flame must not hit the roof", and incomplete-combustion products react with the refractory at high temperature (fetched) — [ispatguru](https://www.ispatguru.com/types-of-burners-in-reheating-furnaces/). Flame impingement erodes refractory locally and makes shell hot spots (snippet) — [BIC Magazine](https://www.bicmagazine.com/resources/sponsored-content/managing-hot-spots-in-primary-reformers/). Glass-furnace patents name crown deterioration as a design concern (snippet) — [Google Patents US10273178](https://patents.google.com/patent/US10273178). Penetrations in a crown need gas-tight sealing and must be drilled without thermal shock (snippet) — [Google Patents US6540508](https://patents.google.com/patent/US6540508)
- **Overheating the charge surface** after a sudden change in roof-burner output (fetched) — [ispatguru](https://www.ispatguru.com/types-of-burners-in-reheating-furnaces/)
- **Burner tiles and blocks:** tiles "suffer from severe thermal shock caused by heavy switching between burning and extinguishing ... [and] tend to crack or peel off" (snippet) — [USPTO PDF 10527283: burner tile, burner and furnace](https://image-ppubs.uspto.gov/dirsearch-public/print/downloadPdf/10527283). Once a burner block fails, "the failure will often rapidly spread", which usually means replacing it (snippet) — [USPTO PDF 12259131: burner block assembly](https://image-ppubs.uspto.gov/dirsearch-public/print/downloadPdf/12259131). Hot gas leaking round the tile's expansion gap can overheat the burner mounting plate or casing and make the retention cleats fail early; Zeeco pre-dries tiles to 360 °C (680 °F) to avoid cracking at start-up (vendor, snippet) — [Zeeco: taking on the tiling puzzle](https://www.zeeco.com/resources/news/taking-on-the-tiling-puzzle); [Zeecolite datasheet (PDF)](https://www.zeeco.com/hubfs/Zeeco-En_June_2020/pdf/Zeecolite-02-20.pdf?hsLang=en)
- **Burner life on a hot lid:** a lid with burners left over molten metal would "shorten [the] service life of the burner", which is why the design has a second lid (fetched) — [Google Patents US10598437](https://patents.google.com/patent/US10598437)
- **Exhaust too hot for metal recuperators** on a lid-fired ladle preheater (about 1270 K preheat) (snippet) — [University of Utah collection](https://collections.lib.utah.edu/details?id=13374)
- **Chimney effect and hot gas hose on top-mounted burners:** "burners inserted vertically have more of a chimney effect issue when you shut down the forge"; "the gas hose gets a lot more hot than I am comfortable with" (snippet) — [IForgeIron thread 77481](https://www.iforgeiron.com/topic/77481-burner-orientation-is-sideways-effective/); [IForgeIron thread 67604](https://www.iforgeiron.com/topic/67604-concept-horizontal-front-mount-burner/)

- **Gas supply to a moving lid:** the ladle-cover burner is fed through a "steel flexible hose" so it can move with the boom; the exhaust stacks connect to the plant flue through flexible tubes; the electrical controls are on the boom (fetched) — [Google Patents US4014532A](https://patents.google.com/patent/US4014532A/en). ### Inferences
- For a lid manifold, the failure modes from comparable appliances are: heat soak and burner life on the lid; heat rising up the burner throats at shutdown; hot gas lines above the lid; cracked burner blocks or tiles from on-off cycling; refractory erosion where a flame touches the lid or walls; and the top of the setting running hotter than the bottom. The precedents for the gas line are a steel flexible hose and a boom or pivot that keeps the lid level.

### Gaps
- **Lid warping:** no source found for steel lid or shell warping on a roof-fired appliance. One burner-block source mentions stresses moving into the mounting structure and warping it, which lets gas bypass, but I could not attribute that sentence to a specific URL with confidence, so it is left out.
- **Flashback** in premix roof burners: no specific roof-burner flashback report found apart from the ribbon-burner explosion anecdote.
- The text of NFPA 86 on flexible connectors and gas piping to moving or opening equipment was not reached. Check the current edition (2023 or 2026) and the authority having jurisdiction.
