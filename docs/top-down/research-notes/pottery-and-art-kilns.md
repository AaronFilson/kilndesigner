# Pottery, ceramic, raku, glass and enameling kilns with gas burners in the lid, crown or hood

Scope note on method: about 30 web searches and fetches (October 2026). The Ceramic Arts Daily forum
(community.ceramicartsdaily.org) returned HTTP 403 to direct fetches, so what is reported from it
comes only from search-result excerpts and is marked that way. I could not view image contents. Where a
page is said to have photos, that comes from its alt text or captions; nobody checked what the
photos actually show. Claims marked "(search excerpt)" come from the search engine's excerpt of a page I
did not open in full.

## Are there commercial gas kilns (top-loaders, top-hat or bell, raku, glass, enameling) with burners in the lid or hood?

### Takeaway
I found no commercial studio pottery, raku, enameling or fusing kiln with its gas burners in the lid
or hood. Every studio gas kiln I checked puts the burners in the floor, base or lower walls and uses
the lid as the flue. The only commercial art-glass product found with a top-mounted burner is the
Canned Heat Glass glory hole: its burner enters tangentially at the top right of a cylindrical chamber.
Downward or crown firing exists in industry: top-fired tunnel kilns, a shuttle kiln with its burners
above the ware, and crown-fired oxy-fuel glass tanks.

### Cited Findings
- Canned Heat Glass Glory Hole 12: "Combustion system: Tangentially mounted from top right side. 3.75" (9.5 cm) round burner", "Approximately 175,000 BTU's"; opening 12 in (30.5 cm) in diameter, chamber depth 24 in (61 cm); "Cut and mortared 2800°F (1538°C) brick", replaceable cast floor and 2 in replaceable heat-retention ring; one set of 6.5 in barn doors; "800lbs (365 kg) including palette"; outside 34 in W x 52 in H x 48.5 in D; "Heats up in under an hour and stays hot with doors wide open." The page names no fuel and no flue. It has a main image and four thumbnails of the product, but I could not see what the photos show. — [Canned Heat Glass, Glory Hole 12](https://www.cannedheatglass.com/product-page/12-glory-hole)
- Canned Heat Glass Glory Hole 10: same tangential top-right mounting with a 2.75 in round burner of about 135,000 BTU (search excerpt). — [Canned Heat Glass, Glory Hole 10](https://www.cannedheatglass.com/product-page/10-brick-hole)
- US 3,854,865, "Kiln for Ceramic Products" (Gordon C. Fay, assigned to Hendryx Engineers; filed 9 November 1973, issued 17 December 1974). It is a shuttle kiln whose floor is made of cars. "Burners are provided in the upper part of the kiln, above the ware." The gases are discharged with enough pressure "to induce a downward flow of gas through the ware". The gas leaves through openings in the car floor, then through horizontal flues low in the side walls and vertical ducts that return some of it to venturi blocks at the burners. "Excess gas is exhausted from the flues through the vertical stacks 74 at the four corners of the kiln." The patent claims the downdraft "positively insures uniform heating of the ware", and that recirculation increases heat transfer "particularly to the bottom of the stacked ware". Fuel is gas or fuel oil, and the burners are "the sole source of heat". — [Google Patents US3854865A](https://patents.google.com/patent/US3854865A/en)
- Rohde TG80 top-loading gas kiln (a typical studio top-loader). A "stable 20kW burner fixed to the underside of the frame", with a "quick-release hose to burner connection". The burner is under the kiln, not in the lid (search excerpt). — [Hot Clay, Rohde TG80](https://www.hot-clay.com/products/rohde-top-loading-gas-kiln-tg-80)
- Olympic Torchbearer top-loading gas kilns. Olympic says updraft vent hoods must sit "approximately 36″ above the top porthole, or enough room to raise the lid": the lid lifts and the exhaust leaves through the top, so the burners are not in the lid (search excerpt). — [Olympic Kilns, Vent Hoods for Gas Kilns](https://www.olympickilns.com/product/vent-hoods-for-gas-kilns/)
- NUTEC Bickley elevator kilns. Burners are "typically positioned at the corners". On the downdraft version an insulated duct couples to the car flue when the car is fully raised (search excerpt). — [NUTEC Bickley, Elevator Kilns](https://www.nutecbickley.com/what-we-do/industrial-kilns-for-ceramics/elevator-kilns)
- Wikipedia describes shuttle kilns with burners "positioned top and bottom on each side, creating a turbulent circular air flow" (search excerpt). — [Wikipedia, Kiln](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kiln)
- A forum excerpt says the "Kilnn" unit uses "a single high velocity burner fired down from a top rear corner on about a 60 cubic foot kiln". The same thread argues that high-velocity burners spread hot gas widely, so chamber shape matters less. The page returned HTTP 403; a separate search for "Kilnn" found nothing. — [Ceramic Arts Daily Community, Burner Placement In Kiln Design](https://community.ceramicartsdaily.org/topic/8482-burner-placement-in-kiln-design/)

### Inferences
- The lid of a studio top-loader carries the flue almost everywhere (Olympic, Rohde, and the electric-to-gas conversions below), so a lid burner would mean moving the exhaust somewhere else, most likely to the floor (downdraft). That is the Hendryx arrangement.
- The Canned Heat glory hole is the closest commercial match to the "half-moon arc" idea: one burner enters from the top and fires tangentially so the flame wraps round the inside of a cylinder. It is a reheating chamber with an open front, not a closed kiln with a flue.

### Gaps
- I found no commercial gas raku, enameling, fusing or slumping kiln with lid or hood burners. The listed makers were not each checked model by model: Geil, Bailey, Laguna/Giffin, Ward, Aim, Euclid's, Seattle Pottery Supply, Sheffield, Larkin, Woodrow, Northern Kilns, Kilns & Furnaces, Furnace Engineering Australia, and the Japanese and Korean makers. The general searches turned up none from them.
- "Kilnn" could not be identified. Neither its maker nor its photos could be found.
- I did not read the full text of US 4,240,788, US 5,667,378 or US 4,014,532. Their details (six rows of six burners, nozzle clogging, burner on a boom) come from search excerpts.

## Are there DIY or studio builds with lid-mounted or crown-mounted burners?

### Takeaway
I found no DIY or studio pottery build with a gas burner through the lid or crown. The DIY raku, trash-can,
fiber and electric-to-gas builds all put the burner low on the side or under the floor and the flue in the
lid. The nearest DIY precedents are a glory hole build and gas forges, where top-mounted, angled burners are common.

### Cited Findings
- Ward Burner Systems' guide to converting electric kilns: burner ports in the floor, with "a pattern ... provided to cut the flue in the top of the kiln" (search excerpt). — [Ward Burner, Converting Electric Kilns](https://wardburner.com/converting-electric/)
- Other electric-to-gas conversions put the burner port low on the side ("one hole is at six o'clock (burner port)") with a chimney on the lid. None found fires through the lid (search excerpt). — [Marian Williams Pottery](https://www.marianwilliamspottery.com/2020/04/05/how-to-convert-an-old-electric-kiln-to-a-gas-fired-kiln/); [CodyO Pottery](http://codyopottery.blogspot.com/2009/10/electric-to-gas-conversion-birth-of-my.html); [Jack Welbourne](https://jackwelbourneceramics.wordpress.com/2015/09/10/converting-an-electric-kiln-to-fire-with-gas/); [Sebastian Mark, downdraft conversion](http://www.sebastianmarkblog.com/2018/07/gas-kiln-conversion-downdraft.html)
- A Clayart dual-fuel conversion ran a small propane burner through a 1 in hole in the floor, with a hole the same size in the lid. Elements failed in the final firing (search excerpt). — [potters.org, "continuing dual fuel kiln story"](http://www.potters.org/subject100050.htm)
- DIY glory hole build: a 45-gallon oil drum shell with a cast refractory burner port, tapered at about 4 degrees included angle so it can be removed, designed round a Giberson-style burner head. Burner position not confirmed (search excerpt). — [metal-arts.co.uk, Gloryhole construction](https://metal-arts.co.uk/gloryhole/index.html)
- Video of a 6 in glory hole with a Giberson 2.5 in "Mini Square" burner. Burner position not checked. — [YouTube](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=vuB8Rz9S7MI)
- Gas forge builders often mount burners through the top of the shell. Forum consensus for round forges is to angle the burner so the flame hits the floor obliquely and sets up a vortex; burners pointing straight down "mix the flames more chaotically" and give less even heat (search excerpt). — [I Forge Iron, burner angle in a cylindrical forge](https://www.iforgeiron.com/topic/14128-burner-angle-in-a-cylindrical-forge/); [I Forge Iron, burner placement](https://www.iforgeiron.com/topic/61646-burner-placement/)
- A top-entry forge burner gives a hot spot: useful for forge-welding Damascus, but "it is hard to get an even heat over the whole knife" (search excerpt). — [BladeForums, burner angle injection](https://www.bladeforums.com/threads/forge-burner-angle-injection.216865/)
- Aiming a naturally aspirated forge burner into a corner sends flame back at the burner, causing back pressure and sputtering; flattening the angle so the flame strikes the wall and turns along the floor cures it. Aiming straight at a wall makes a hot spot there (search excerpt). — [I Forge Iron, burner placement](https://www.iforgeiron.com/topic/61646-burner-placement/)
- On swirl: "Most people falsely assume that atmospheric swirl takes a lot of angle; it doesn't." One Australian builder found a side-angled burner gave a one-sided hot spot and little swirl (search excerpt). — [BladeForums, burner positioning](https://www.bladeforums.com/threads/burner-positioning-and-other-forge-design-considerations.1581859/); [Australian Blade Forums](https://www.australianbladeforums.info/forum/have-your-say/general-blade-discussion/forge-wip/34655-)

### Inferences
- The forge forums supply the most relevant field experience for the user's idea: top-entry burners set at a shallow tangent give swirl and evener heat, while burners pointing straight down make a hot spot under the flame. A cast manifold that splits the flame into two opposed half-moon arcs round the perimeter would be a multi-port tangential arrangement of this kind. I found no build that does exactly that.

### Gaps
- Reddit (r/Pottery, r/Ceramics, r/raku, r/kilns), Instagram, Flickr and Pinterest: searches returned no matching posts, and none were fetched.
- The specific Ceramic Arts Daily threads could not be fetched because of the 403.
- I did not check Olsen's *The Kiln Book* or Rhodes's *Kilns* for crown-burner designs.

## Do any "downfired", "top-fired" or "overhead-fired" pottery kilns exist, and how were they drafted?

### Takeaway
Yes, but in industry, not in studio pottery. Top-fired tunnel kilns, Hoffmann kilns fed through the roof,
and the Hendryx shuttle kiln all fire from above and exhaust low, through or under the setting
(downdraft). I found no studio pottery kiln described as "top-fired" or "downfired".

### Cited Findings
- Hendryx shuttle kiln: burners above the ware, gas driven down through the setting, exhaust through openings in the car floor to side flues and corner stacks; uniform heating claimed (see above). — [Google Patents US3854865A](https://patents.google.com/patent/US3854865A/en)
- US 4,240,788, "Intermittent top firing tunnel kiln equipped with a burner having a ceramic air nozzle": liquid-fuel burners built into the ceiling. The excerpt says the nozzles "can clog with carbon at high temperatures, which requires much labor for daily maintenance" (search excerpt). — [USPTO 4240788](https://image-ppubs.uspto.gov/dirsearch-public/print/downloadPdf/4240788)
- US 5,667,378, "Low profile kiln apparatus": "a plurality of high velocity gas burners located in this zone in the roof of the kiln", reportedly six rows of six across the width, angled toward the load, each row a temperature zone with its own thermocouple (search excerpt). — [USPTO 5667378](https://image-ppubs.uspto.gov/dirsearch-public/print/downloadPdf/5667378)
- Hoffmann kilns: "Fire holes with covers are provided to feed fuel at the top of each chamber". Operators dropped small measured amounts of crushed coal, and bricks set in the shafts scatter it through the height of the setting (search excerpt). — [Resilience.org, Rings of fire: Hoffmann kilns](https://www.resilience.org/stories/2009-11-16/rings-fire-hoffmann-kilns/); [Chest of Books, Hoffmann and Licht kiln](https://chestofbooks.com/architecture/Pottery/A-Continuous-Kilns-With-Solid-Fuel-Hoffmann-And-Licht-Kiln.html)
- A Chinese equipment vendor says the many coal holes in a Hoffmann kiln leak gas, citing 526 coal holes in one Hoffmann kiln against 127 in a tunnel kiln. This is a commercial source; treat with caution. — [sysdf brick machine](http://www.sysdf-brickmachine.com/news/Tunnel-Kiln-Hoffman-Kiln-Comparison.html)
- Wood-fired "downdraft barrel kiln": a chimney outlet welded at the base, fire built in the barrel, then the lid put on but left "cracked so that the air can be drawn in and down". Fire is at the top and the exhaust at the bottom, but there is no burner (search excerpt). — [The Pottery Wheel, 7 Ways to Build a Kiln](https://thepotterywheel.com/build-a-kiln-for-pottery/)
- Ordinary studio downdraft kilns fire from the floor: "the fire enters from the bottom, shoots up to the top of the kiln, comes back down to the bottom and then exits through two flues at the base" (search excerpt). — [Jon Rawlings Pottery](https://www.jonrawlingspottery.com/blog/firing-the-gas-kiln-what-ive-learned-so-far)
- High-velocity burner behaviour, relevant to firing from above: jets approach 500 mph and entrain up to 10 times their own volume of kiln gas. Burners set back from the hot face caused hot spots in the lower levels of the setting, fixed by fitting a ceramic tube in front of each burner (search excerpt). — [Ceramic Industry, Kiln Connection: High-Velocity Burners](https://www.ceramicindustry.com/articles/92782-kiln-connection-high-velocity-burners/)

### Inferences
- Every top-fired precedent found exhausts low (downdraft through the floor or car). With burners and flue both in the lid, the hot gas would short-circuit across the top of the kiln, as the raku guides warn below.

### Gaps
- I found no studio-scale top-fired pottery kiln with firing records or a cone map.

## Do raku kilns or converted electric kilns ever take the burner through the lid?

### Takeaway
Not in any source found. Raku kilns of every kind (trash-can, barrel, fiber top-hat, flowerpot, commercial
Olympic models) put the burner low on the side or under the floor, and use the lid or top only as the vent.

### Cited Findings
- Standard trash-can raku: a burner port cut "a couple of inches from the base", with "a matching vent in the lid" (search excerpt). — [The Pottery Wheel, How to Make a Raku Kiln](https://thepotterywheel.com/how-to-make-a-raku-kiln/)
- Ceramic Arts Daily thread on a raku kiln made from fiber, wire and a trash-can lid: hole near the bottom, weed burner (search excerpt). — [Ceramic Arts Daily Community](https://community.ceramicartsdaily.org/topic/13362-going-to-make-raku-kiln-fiber-wire-and-trash-can-lid/)
- Clayart 1999 "trash can raku kiln" thread: weed burners are slow for some potters, while one says theirs reaches 1900 F in about 15 minutes; another warns "VERY CAREFUL" because weed burners have no safety features (search excerpt). — [potters.org](http://www.potters.org/subject09215.htm)
- A Ceramics Monthly raku design brings the burner in from the side and draws the flame up through a 3 in flue drilled in the lid (search excerpt). — [Ceramics Monthly, Raku Kiln Design Revisited](https://ceramicartsnetwork.org/ceramics-monthly/ceramics-monthly-article/Tips-and-Tools-Raku-Kiln-Design-Revisited-213902)
- Raku guides warn "you don't want the flame to go in and straight out the top!" and suggest shelves or bricks to direct the flame. One potter found that a burner angled at 45 degrees rather than 90 made the flame "scoop around the inside of the kiln" (search excerpt). — [The Pottery Wheel, How to Raku Fire](https://thepotterywheel.com/how-to-raku-fire/); [Lucktaylor Ceramics](https://www.lucktaylorceramics.co.uk/blog/howtobuildarakukiln)
- Expandable raku kiln patent: burners centred under the floor (search excerpt). — [Google Patents US9360254B2](https://patents.google.com/patent/US9360254)
- Olympic 23 Raku is sold as a gas raku kiln for natural gas or propane; I did not check where its burner sits. — [Kilnfrog, Olympic 23 Raku](https://kilnfrog.com/products/olympic-kiln-23-raku-gas-fired)
- UK HSE: raku kilns should not be run indoors, and the lid should be opened far side first to cut the risk of flash fire (search excerpt). — [HSE, Safety at Raku kilns](https://www.hse.gov.uk/foi/internalops/sims/manuf/3_09_11.htm)

### Inferences
- Raku lids are lifted hot and often, so a burner in the lid would have to lift with it: a moving hose, plus flame supervision on a moving part. That may be part of why no examples appear. I found no source that says so.

### Gaps
- Raku books (Branfman and others) were not checked.

## Are there top-hat or bell kilns that carry their burners on the lifting hood, and how are the gas lines handled?

### Takeaway
I found no confirmed studio or industrial top-hat or bell kiln with its burners on the moving hood, and no
documented flexible gas-line arrangement for one. Industrial makers describe burners at the corners, and the
moving interfaces they document are exhaust ducts that couple when the car is in place, not fuel lines.

### Cited Findings
- Bell-type kilns: "a hood construction, which is lowered to a fixed furnace or kiln car". Burner location is not given (search excerpt). — [Kiln-Tech (German Kiln Technology), Bell type kilns](https://www.kiln-tech.com/products/kiln-types/bell-type-kilns)
- NUTEC Bickley Carbell (top-hat) kilns are built for a repeatable seal between kiln and car, with burners "typically positioned at the corners" on small and medium sizes. The page does not say whether the corner burners are on the hood or the base (search excerpt). — [NUTEC Bickley, Carbell Kilns](https://www.nutecbickley.com/what-we-do/industrial-kilns-for-ceramics/carbell-kilns); [NUTEC Bickley, Elevator Kilns](https://www.nutecbickley.com/what-we-do/industrial-kilns-for-ceramics/elevator-kilns)
- An Australian burner supplier lists flexible hoses "for non-permanent situations" (search excerpt). — [Gasco, Ceramic Burners](https://www.gasco.net.au/combust.com.au/ceramics/ceramics.html)
- Rohde's top-loader uses a quick-release hose connection to a burner fixed under the frame. — [Hot Clay, Rohde TG80](https://www.hot-clay.com/products/rohde-top-loading-gas-kiln-tg-80)
- Kiln vent hoods that lift (counterweight pulley, about 10 ft of flexible duct) are the only lifting-hood plumbing found, and they carry exhaust, not fuel (search excerpt). — [Sheffield Pottery, Vent-a-Kiln](https://www.sheffield-pottery.com/Vent-a-Kiln-Kiln-Venting-System-s/75.htm)
- Carbolite HB is a top-hat furnace whose hood lifts vertically off a fixed hearth, but it is electric. — [Carbolite HB](https://www.carbolite.com/products/chamber-furnaces/industrial-furnaces/hb/)

### Inferences
- Moving the kiln car rather than the burner-carrying hood is the usual industrial answer. The Hendryx shuttle and NUTEC elevator designs keep the burners on the fixed structure and move the ware.

### Gaps
- No manual or photo was found showing gas hoses following a lifting hood on a kiln. Steel-industry bell annealers and ladle preheaters may be better places to look for that detail; they were not researched here beyond the ladle patent.

## Are there glass-fusing, slumping or enameling kilns, or glass furnaces and glory holes, with top-mounted gas burners?

### Takeaway
Glory holes: yes, with Canned Heat's top tangential burner confirmed. Industrial glass melting tanks: yes,
crown-mounted down-fired oxy-fuel burners are well documented. Gas fusing, slumping and enameling kilns with
lid burners: none found. Gas is rare in warm glass at all, because control is the problem.

### Cited Findings
- Canned Heat glory holes: tangential burner from the top right (see the catalogue). — [Canned Heat Glass](https://www.cannedheatglass.com/product-page/12-glory-hole)
- Sandia/DOE Glass Furnace Combustion and Melting Research Facility: can be fired "(1) using a single large burner mounted on the front wall, (2) by six burners in a staggered/opposed arrangement, three in each breast wall, and (3) by down-fired burners mounted in the crown in any combination" (search excerpt). — [OSTI 919106](https://www.osti.gov/servlets/purl/919106); [Sandia](https://www.sandia.gov/research/publications/details/glass-furnace-combustion-and-melting-research-facility-2004-08-01/)
- Roof-mounted oxy-fuel patents: the burners are "directed downwards at an angle greater than 45°" to the batch surface. The patents note that glass furnaces were conventionally not fitted with crown burners, and that crown burners have been added over the unmelted batch to boost regenerative furnaces (search excerpt). — [US 6,705,118](https://image-ppubs.uspto.gov/dirsearch-public/print/downloadPdf/6705118); [US 6,705,117](https://image-ppubs.uspto.gov/dirsearch-public/print/downloadPdf/6705117); [US 6,422,041](https://image-ppubs.uspto.gov/dirsearch-public/print/downloadPdf/6422041); [US 6,540,508 (installing roof burners)](https://image-ppubs.uspto.gov/dirsearch-public/print/downloadPdf/6540508)
- A crown burner patent: "directed toward the upper surface of the bath, from the top downward", placed away from the main gas-movement zone (search excerpt). — [US 11,807,567](https://image-ppubs.uspto.gov/dirsearch-public/print/downloadPdf/11807567)
- Crown-corrosion patents: auxiliary burners are typically set high on a wall near the crown so the combustion products sweep along the crown (search excerpt). — [US 8,640,500](https://image-ppubs.uspto.gov/dirsearch-public/print/downloadPdf/8640500)
- Gas for fusing and slumping: a WarmGlass forum thread reports opposing expert views. One says gas kilns "cannot be precisely controlled to the extent needed for fusing, slumping and casting"; another says a precise controller would fix it, at about $3,000. Both views are second-hand and undated (search excerpt). — [WarmGlass, natural gas kilns](https://warmglass.com/phpBB3/viewtopic.php?t=101)
- Electric fusing kilns usually carry their elements in the lid. One builder heats from the top because it "gives the glass a better gloss finish". With elements only in the lid, stacking shelves heats unevenly (search excerpt). — [Instructables, home-made fusing kiln](https://www.instructables.com/Home-made-electric-glass-fusing-kiln/); [Glasshoppa, Choosing Your First Kiln](https://www.glasshoppa.com/FirstKiln.html); [Skutt, Glass 101](https://skutt.com/skutt-resources/education/glass-101/)

### Inferences
- Electric fusing kilns already heat from the top, so a gas kiln fired from the lid would heat glass the same way. I found no gas version.

### Gaps
- No gas-fired enameling kiln of any layout was found. The studio glass pot furnaces I looked at did not show crown burners; studio furnace makers were not checked one by one.

## What firing results, evenness problems, hot or cold spots, and failures have been reported?

### Takeaway
There are no firing reports for lid-fired pottery kilns, because none were found. The relevant evidence is
indirect: forge users find that burners pointing straight down make hot spots, while tangential entry gives
swirl. Industrial top-fired kilns report nozzle clogging, and claim uniform heating when downdraft and
recirculation are used. Small updraft kilns run hot or cold from top to bottom.

### Cited Findings
- Top-entry forge burners: a hot spot directly under the burner, and even heat over long work is hard to get (search excerpt). — [BladeForums](https://www.bladeforums.com/threads/forge-burner-angle-injection.216865/)
- Burners pointing straight down give less even temperatures than angled vortex firing; aiming into a corner causes back pressure and sputtering (search excerpt). — [I Forge Iron 14128](https://www.iforgeiron.com/topic/14128-burner-angle-in-a-cylindrical-forge/); [I Forge Iron 61646](https://www.iforgeiron.com/topic/61646-burner-placement/)
- Top-firing tunnel kiln: ceiling burner nozzles clog with carbon and need daily maintenance (liquid fuel) (search excerpt). — [USPTO 4240788](https://image-ppubs.uspto.gov/dirsearch-public/print/downloadPdf/4240788)
- Hendryx top-fired shuttle kiln: uniformity claimed; recirculation said to improve heating "particularly to the bottom of the stacked ware". These are the patentee's claims, not measurements. — [Google Patents US3854865A](https://patents.google.com/patent/US3854865A/en)
- High-velocity burners: set-back burners caused hot spots in the lower levels of the setting (search excerpt). — [Ceramic Industry](https://www.ceramicindustry.com/articles/92782-kiln-connection-high-velocity-burners/)
- A small floor-fired kiln with the burner from a Zen Raku kiln fired cone 6 at the top and cone 10 at the bottom. The suggested fixes were raising the bottom shelf, opening the top more, and angling the flame (search excerpt). — [potters.org, how do I even out the firing?](http://www.potters.org/subject16576.htm)
- Gas glass kilns: control is the main doubt (see WarmGlass above). — [WarmGlass](https://warmglass.com/phpBB3/viewtopic.php?t=101)
- Unattended burner blow-out before the interior is red hot can cause an explosion, so flame-failure shut-off is recommended. This matters more for a burner that moves with a lid (search excerpt). — [Dan Palmer Ceramics](https://danpalmerceramics.com/2024/05/15/building-and-rebuilding-a-better-gas-kiln/)

### Inferences
- From the forge evidence: a lid manifold firing straight down would probably overheat the top of the setting and leave the floor cold. Tangential half-moon arcs along the wall, exhausting at the floor, match the arrangements reported to give the evenest heat. This is an inference from analogues, not a tested result.

### Gaps
- No cone packs, thermocouple surveys or failure reports exist for a lid-fired studio gas kiln in the sources found.
- Effects on lid weight and construction (burner blocks, a manifold casting, and heat soak on the lid hardware) are not documented for any art kiln.
