# Lid-fired kilns must flip the draft downward

No studio pottery kiln was found, commercial or homebuilt, that fires from burners in its lid. Every gas top-loader, raku kiln and electric-to-gas conversion surveyed puts the burners at the floor and uses the lid as the flue. Top-down gas firing is still real and well established elsewhere. Knife forges fire straight down from the roof. A commercial glory hole fires tangentially from the top. An 1893 glory-hole patent spreads a roof flame into a sheet. Steel-mill ladle covers carry burners round their rim, aimed tangentially. A Japanese melting furnace puts three burners in its lid. Brick tunnel kilns, glass tanks, steel reheat furnaces and steam-methane reformers all fire through their roofs. The closest precedent to the user's idea is a 1977 ladle-preheater patent: several burners round the edge of a removable refractory cover, firing tangentially and fed through steel flexible hose. No patent or build was found that combines a kiln lid, a perimeter arc layout and a cast manifold. Every precedent that fires down into a stacked load exhausts at or near the floor, and even then the top of the load heats faster than the bottom. The proposed two half-moon arcs reproduce the pair of counter-rotating vortices that forge builders get from a straight-down burner and work to avoid, although industry does use opposed-rotation roof burners on purpose to even out heat. The design is buildable in principle as a downdraft kiln. That means power (blower) burners, a raw-gas manifold kept behind the insulation with refractory or ceramic nozzles at the hot face, a heat-safe gas connection to the moving lid, and flame supervision interlocked to the lid's position. No published code covers burners on a lid that moves. No firing data, cone map or failure report exists for any lid-fired studio kiln, and much of the forum evidence below was read only through search-engine excerpts, so every claim about how evenly this design would fire is untested.

## Top-down gas firing thrives everywhere except the potter's lid

Studio gas kilns are consistent about where the burners go. Rohde's TG80 top-loader has a **20 kW burner fixed to the underside of the frame** with a quick-release hose ([Hot Clay](https://www.hot-clay.com/products/rohde-top-loading-gas-kiln-tg-80)). Olympic tells buyers of its Torchbearer kilns to hang the vent hood about 36 in above the top porthole, high enough to raise the lid, because the exhaust leaves through the top ([Olympic Kilns](https://www.olympickilns.com/product/vent-hoods-for-gas-kilns/)). Ward's guide to converting electric kilns puts the burner ports in the floor and supplies "a pattern ... to cut the flue in the top of the kiln" ([Ward Burner](https://wardburner.com/converting-electric/)). Raku kilns do the same: the standard trash-can design cuts a burner port "a couple of inches from the base" with "a matching vent in the lid" ([The Pottery Wheel](https://thepotterywheel.com/how-to-make-a-raku-kiln/)). A patent for a portable gas-fired art-pottery kiln puts a central tapered flue, not a burner, in the lid ([US3752643A](https://patents.google.com/patent/US3752643A/en)).

The only hint of a studio-scale exception is a single forum excerpt describing a "Kilnn" unit with "a single high velocity burner fired down from a top rear corner on about a 60 cubic foot kiln". The page was blocked and no maker could be found ([Ceramic Arts Daily](https://community.ceramicartsdaily.org/topic/8482-burner-placement-in-kiln-design/)). Electric glass-fusing kilns do heat from the lid, and one builder says top heat "gives the glass a better gloss finish". Once shelves are stacked, though, lid-only heat becomes uneven ([Instructables](https://www.instructables.com/Home-made-electric-glass-fusing-kiln/); [Glasshoppa](https://www.glasshoppa.com/FirstKiln.html)).

Forges are where the user's "like a forge" intuition comes from. Owners describe Majestic's knifemaker forges as firing straight down from the top. That heats steel quickly but leaves "a serious hot spot" ([BladeForums](https://bladeforums.com/threads/does-anyone-have-or-have-experience-with-a-majestic-forge.777023)). Forum veterans put the vertical mount down mostly to history, "that's the way most of the first gas forges came out", and contrast it with DIY plans that angle the burner to about 2 o'clock ([IForgeIron, Forges 101](https://www.iforgeiron.com/topic/47439-forges-101/page/19/); [IForgeIron, burner placement](https://www.iforgeiron.com/topic/61646-burner-placement/)).

Glass offers the cleanest small-scale precedents. Canned Heat Glass's Glory Hole 12 has one **3.75 in burner "tangentially mounted from top right side", about 175,000 Btu/h (51 kW)**. Its chamber is 12 in across and 24 in deep, lined with 2800 °F brick, and the unit weighs 800 lb ([Canned Heat Glass](https://www.cannedheatglass.com/product-page/12-glory-hole)). C. D. Trimble's 1893 glory-hole patent fires down through a slot in the roof. A cast "flame spreader" with sharp inner edges turns the jet into a vertical sheet, and the exhaust leaves through "smoke arches" at the foot of the side walls. That makes it a top-fired, bottom-exhausted appliance, run on oil and compressed air ([US509146](https://patents.google.com/patent/US509146)). Industrial glass tanks mount oxy-fuel burners in the crown, aimed down at the batch at more than 45° ([US6705118](https://image-ppubs.uspto.gov/dirsearch-public/print/downloadPdf/6705118)). Sandia's research furnace can fire through "down-fired burners mounted in the crown" ([OSTI 919106](https://www.osti.gov/servlets/purl/919106)). Fitting those burners means drilling the crown without thermal shock and sealing each burner block gas-tight ([US6540508](https://patents.google.com/patent/US6540508)).

Metal processing supplies the closest match to a burner lid. Carl Holley's ladle preheater (filed 1976, granted 1977) projects a burner into a concave, refractory-lined cover laid on the ladle mouth. In the embodiment of Figs. 5–6, **several burners "along the periphery of said cover" project flames "substantially tangentially"** to heat large ladles more evenly. Gas reaches the burners through a needle valve, a "steel flexible hose", a gauge and an inspirator, all carried on a boom whose pivot keeps the cover level ([US4014532A](https://patents.google.com/patent/US4014532A/en)). Aisin Takaoka's hybrid melting furnace (granted 2020) mounts **three burners in the lid around its axis**, firing down into the crucible. It also has a second lid with no burners, because leaving the burner lid over molten metal would "shorten the service life of the burner" ([US10598437](https://patents.google.com/patent/US10598437)). Conventional crucible furnaces do the opposite: they fire tangentially at the base and avoid aiming at the crucible, which cracks it ([US4455016](https://image-ppubs.uspto.gov/dirsearch-public/print/downloadPdf/4455016)).

Industrial ceramics and steel make the most use of roof firing. Hendryx Engineers' 1974 shuttle kiln puts its burners "in the upper part of the kiln, above the ware" and drives the gas down through the setting and out through the car floor ([US3854865A](https://patents.google.com/patent/US3854865A/en)). Swindell Dressler's low-profile tunnel kiln uses about **72 high-velocity burners, all in the roof** ([US5667378A](https://patents.google.com/patent/US5667378A/en)). Shinagawa Refractories hung ceramic-nozzled burners from the ceiling of a tunnel kiln firing at 1350–1650 °C ([US4240788A](https://patents.google.com/patent/US4240788A/en)). Hoffmann ring kilns were fed crushed coal through capped "feed holes" in the roof ([Low-tech Magazine](https://solar.lowtechmagazine.com/2009/10/rings-of-fire-hoffmann-kilns/)). Steel reheat furnaces hang flat-flame burners in the roof, where they "spread the flame over the roof surface in a thin layer" so the roof becomes "the chief radiating surface" ([ispatguru](https://www.ispatguru.com/types-of-burners-in-reheating-furnaces/)).

The counter-example matters just as much. Where industry needs tight load uniformity in a batch furnace, it fires from the side, over and under the load, not from the roof. L&L expects **±25 °F** from six burners in a car-bottom furnace ([L&L](https://www.llfurnace.com/news/press-release-order-for-gas-fired-car-bottom-furnace-for-heat-treating-steel-pipe-and-fittings)), and NUTEC Bickley uses 11 pulse-fired zones staggered above and below the load ([Heat Treat Today](https://www.heattreattoday.com/?p=30214)).

## Every top-fired stacked load exhausts at the floor

Hot gas rises. A closed kiln is therefore under positive pressure at the top and negative pressure at the bottom, with a neutral plane between ([Designing Buildings Wiki](https://designingbuildings.co.uk/wiki/Stack_effect)). Burners in the lid would sit in the most positive-pressure part of the chamber. Finkelnburg warns that where kiln gas leaks out under pressure, "gases hot enough to cause severe burns can flow out and those gases may be invisible" ([Ceramics Monthly](https://ceramicartsnetwork.org/ceramics-monthly/ceramics-monthly-article/Techno-File-Kiln-Atmosphere)). Studio kilns work with buoyancy rather than against it. Updraft kilns fire up from the floor. Downdraft kilns send the flame up, then down through the ware to a floor flue, and practitioners and an Indonesian university study report this as the evener and more efficient arrangement ([Ceramic Arts Network](https://ceramicartsnetwork.org/daily/article/an-introduction-to-fuel-burning-kilns/); [UGM repository](https://etd.repository.ugm.ac.id/penelitian/detail/63047)). How badly an updraft can stratify shows in one small floor-fired kiln that reached **cone 6 at the top and cone 10 at the bottom** ([potters.org](http://www.potters.org/subject16576.htm)).

Every precedent found that fires down into a stacked load reverses the flow, so the gas leaves at the bottom. Hendryx's kiln discharges its burners with enough pressure "to induce a downward flow of gas through the ware". The gas then leaves through openings in the car floor to low side flues and four corner stacks, and some of it returns to the burners. The patent claims this "positively insures uniform heating" ([US3854865A](https://patents.google.com/patent/US3854865A/en)). Top-fired reformers put a refractory exhaust tunnel on the floor **directly under each burner row** ([EP3182003A1](https://patents.google.com/patent/EP3182003)). A recent patent on downdraft periodic kilns injects gas through the crown and exhausts it through flue openings in the hearth (search summary) ([US11168941](https://image-ppubs.uspto.gov/dirsearch-public/print/downloadPdf/11168941)).

Downdraft alone does not cure stratification. Fives North American's patent on roof-fired brick kilns says that projecting flames downward "causes the stacks of bricks to become heated more quickly near the top than the bottom". Its fix splits the combustion air between "spin" air for a short flame and "forward" air for a long one, which moves the hottest band up or down the stack without changing total heat input ([US7637739B2](https://patents.google.com/patent/US7637739B2/en)). A steel-furnace patent concedes that roof burners give even heat across the width but that "a uniform temperature distribution cannot be adjusted in the lower furnace" with them ([Justia 20150176098](https://patents.justia.com/patent/20150176098)). Reformers heat their tubes unevenly because more flue gas enters the floor tunnels near the open end than near the closed end ([Justia 10711996](https://patents.justia.com/patent/10711996)).

Industry pushes heat downward with jet momentum, not buoyancy. Riedhammer's tunnel kiln fires vertical jets at **80–100 m/s** through 25 mm nozzles. Curb-shaped deflector stones turn the jets into a circulation across the kiln, avoiding "local overheating" ([US4368036A](https://patents.google.com/patent/US4368036A/en)). High-velocity burners approach 500 mph and draw in up to ten times their own volume of kiln gas ([Ceramic Industry](https://www.ceramicindustry.com/articles/92782-kiln-connection-high-velocity-burners/)). The strongest case for the user's idea follows from that: one kiln-forum thread argues that high-velocity burners spread hot gas so widely that the shape of the chamber matters less ([Ceramic Arts Daily](https://community.ceramicartsdaily.org/topic/8482-burner-placement-in-kiln-design/)). Even so, Swindell Dressler fires its 72 roof burners only over bricks set **no more than two high (a load of 18 in or less)**. It also sweeps an oscillating combustion front across the load, because static firing left hot spots ([US5667378A](https://patents.google.com/patent/US5667378A/en)). In industry, roof firing is a technique for shallow, open settings. A tall periodic stack of shelves is a harder problem.

Holley's ladle cover is the instructive exception: it carries both the burners and the flue ([US4014532A](https://patents.google.com/patent/US4014532A/en)). That works because the job is heating the lining of an empty vessel, which the gas washes on its way down and back up. A kiln full of shelves would short-circuit instead, the failure raku guides warn about: "you don't want the flame to go in and straight out the top!" ([The Pottery Wheel](https://thepotterywheel.com/how-to-raku-fire/)).

Positive pressure at the lid also rules out natural-draft burners. Charles A. Hones, a maker of venturi burners, explains that a venturi draws in only part of its combustion air, the rest coming from secondary air, and "this is the principal reason why no Venturi burner will operate properly in a sealed-in combustion chamber" ([Hones](https://charlesahones.com/wp-content/uploads/2019/08/Basic-Venturi-Need-4-Page-8-2019.pdf)). The top of a kiln has back pressure, not the slight suction a venturi port needs. Power burners avoid the problem, and Ward notes that with power burners the chimney only has to clear the building ([Ward, forced air vs venturi](https://www.wardburner.com/technical-info/forced-air-vs-venturi-burners-2/)). If natural draft is kept, the floor flue needs a real chimney. Olsen and Ward size it at three times the downward pull plus a third of the horizontal pull ([Ward, Draft](https://www.wardburner.com/draft-important/); [Ceramic Arts Daily](https://community.ceramicartsdaily.org/topic/17145-chimney-entrance-reduction/)). Ward makes the exit flue equal in area to all the burner ports together, and warns that an undersized flue causes "back-pressure, safety-valve shutdowns, burned thermocouples, stalling" ([Ward, Draft](https://www.wardburner.com/draft-important/)).

Where the floor flue sits also decides whether the swirl from the arcs survives. A German crucible-furnace patent found that only a tangential inlet *and* a tangential outlet, turning the same way at different heights, gave an "orderly" swirl; a tangential inlet alone did worse ([DEP0001754BA](https://patents.google.com/patent/DEP0001754BA/en)). By that logic a single central floor flue would fight a perimeter swirl, while openings set round the floor's edge and lined up with the rotation would carry it on. That is an inference no kiln test has checked.

### Reduction would run weakest where the burners sit

Reduction in a gas kiln depends on chamber pressure. Potters say "pressurizing happens from the top down" and set the damper until flame shows at both the top and bottom spy holes ([CAD 21224](https://community.ceramicartsdaily.org/topic/21224-getting-reduction-at-top-of-gas-kiln/); [CAD 17553](https://community.ceramicartsdaily.org/topic/17553-downdraft-reduction-balance-question/)). In a floor-fired kiln, ware nearest the burner ports comes out "slightly less reduced", because secondary air enters there ([CAD 28621](https://community.ceramicartsdaily.org/topic/28621-uneven-firing-in-small-gas-kiln/); [CAD 6282](https://community.ceramicartsdaily.org/topic/6282-reduction-s/)). Moving the burners to the lid moves that under-reduced zone to the top shelf, which is also where the pressure is highest.

One source on pressure balance warns that over-closing the damper can drop the neutral plane below the burner ports, so that "flue products laden with carbon monoxide will vent from the burner ports". It adds that a flue outlet set low relative to the ports makes this worse. Which page in that set of results said this was not pinned down ([Digitalfire](https://digitalfire.com/burnerbook/index.php)). A lid-fired downdraft is, by construction, a kiln with its flue far below its burner ports. During reduction, CO would leak through the burner ports and the lid seal unless the burners are sealed power burners and the floor flue keeps the top of the chamber near neutral pressure.

The flat-flame burners that spread heat best across a roof are poor tools for reduction. Fives says its 4832 is "not stable on stoichiometric air/gas ratio" in tight, cold furnaces until about **1400 °F (760 °C)**, and the burner is designed to run with excess air ([Fives 4832](https://webasset.fivesgroup.com/Energy_Combustion/Products_-_NA/Flat_Flame/4832_bul-North%20American%20Flat%20Flame%20Bulletin.pdf)). Reduction also has a time window: "full reduction effects in glazes can only be obtained before the glaze melts and glosses over", and continuous reduction makes CO that has to be ventilated ([Ceramics Monthly](https://ceramicartsnetwork.org/ceramics-monthly/ceramics-monthly-article/Techno-File-Kiln-Atmosphere)). A 2023 incident at a lumber dry kiln in Hixon, BC shows what is at stake: CO reached occupied spaces, three people went to hospital, and "there were no O2 or CO sensors in the kiln or occupied workspaces" ([Technical Safety BC](https://www.technicalsafetybc.ca/regulatory-resources/incident-investigations/sawmill-workers-treated-for-carbon-monoxide-exposure-from-kiln-operations)).

## Two half-moon arcs recreate a vortex split forge builders avoid

The two half-moon arcs have an unflattering forge ancestor. When a burner fires straight down from the top, the jet hits the floor and splits "into two swirls of left and right hand spirals". One forge with that layout could only be run turned low, because a stronger flame would "impinge on the stock" ([IForgeIron, burner angle](https://www.iforgeiron.com/topic/54578-school-me-on-burner-angle-please/); [IForgeIron, burner placement](https://www.iforgeiron.com/topic/61646-burner-placement/)). A ribbon burner mounted "from the top blowing strait down" did the same: "the flame came down to the floor then divided and had two swirling vortexes" ([IForgeIron, D-shape forge](https://www.iforgeiron.com/topic/79529-optimal-ribbon-burner-angle-on-d-shape-forge/)). Forge builders treat the split as the defect of top mounting, because the landing jet leaves a hot stripe and "bisect[s] the work area" ([IForgeIron, burner placement](https://www.iforgeiron.com/topic/61646-burner-placement/)). Their usual cure is a single vortex. They angle the burner so the flame lands obliquely near the edge of the floor, sometimes through a port ground slightly elliptical, and credit this with evener heat and longer refractory life ([IForgeIron, cylindrical forge](https://www.iforgeiron.com/topic/14128-burner-angle-in-a-cylindrical-forge/); [IForgeIron, burner location](https://www.iforgeiron.com/topic/32632-burner-location/)). They also warn that swirl is "often overemphasized" ([IForgeIron, heat distribution](https://www.iforgeiron.com/topic/24378-on-heat-distribution-propane-forge/)). One builder adds that with ribbon burners "positioning is less critical", because combustion finishes at the burner face ([IForgeIron, ribbon burner](https://www.iforgeiron.com/topic/39568-ribbon-burner/)).

Industry, on the other hand, uses opposed rotation deliberately. Tenova places clockwise and counter-clockwise TR flat-flame burners on the same roof, which the maker says "ensures a highly uniform heat flux distribution over the charge" ([Tenova](https://tenova.com/technologies/tr-flat-flame-roof-burners)). A batch coil annealer fires side-wall jets into alternating gaps, so gas circles neighbouring covers in opposite directions. Even so, it accepts **about 200 °F of variation** across a cover, and its scale mock-up held 1480 °F with a 1580 °F peak next to the jet ([US4142712A](https://patents.google.com/patent/US4142712A/en)). Opposed rotation works in these cases because each rotating flow heats its own load or its own patch of roof.

That frames the real choice for the two arcs. If both arcs turn the same way, they make one swirl with a slow-moving core, leaving the centre of the setting and the floor to radiation and the downdraft. If the arcs leave a common feed point and run in opposite directions, the two streams collide head-on at the far side and form a stagnation zone. That is the forge's split vortex with the hot spot moved from the floor to the wall opposite the feed. Both outcomes are inferences; nobody has measured either in a kiln. Forming the arcs at the lid does have one real advantage over a single straight-down burner: it avoids the hot stripe where a jet lands on the floor.

Either way the flame is meant to hug the wall, so the outer pots on the top shelf take the impingement. The analogues are blunt. One hobby caster's flame "burnt a hole in the crucible, right where the flame from the burner hits it" ([HomeModelEngineMachinist](https://homemodelenginemachinist.com/threads/hot-water-heater-furnace.19489/page-2)). In down-fired reformers, weak flame momentum lets flames bend back onto the tubes, coking them and overheating their fired side ([US10088154](https://image-ppubs.uspto.gov/dirsearch-public/print/downloadPdf/10088154)), and unchecked impingement progresses to scale, bulging and pinhole leaks ([John Zink](https://www.johnzink.com/resources-training/knowledge-hub/understanding-and-preventing-flame-impingement-on-process-tubes)). Steel reheat practice adds three rules: the flame "must not hit the roof", products of incomplete combustion attack the refractory, and a sudden change in burner output can overheat the surface of the load ([ispatguru](https://www.ispatguru.com/types-of-burners-in-reheating-furnaces/)).

Radiation is the lid's best argument. At furnace temperatures radiant transfer scales with T⁴, and according to Trinks it "dominates heat transfer between any two bodies not in intimate contact" ([US4045211](https://image-ppubs.uspto.gov/dirsearch-public/print/downloadPdf/4045211)). Flat-flame burners exist to turn a roof into an even radiant panel. Fives says its 4832 converts "the refractory expanse of a furnace wall or roof into a uniform heat radiating surface" and limits hot spots "on work as close as 12 inches away" ([Fives 4832](https://webasset.fivesgroup.com/Energy_Combustion/Products_-_NA/Flat_Flame/4832_bul-North%20American%20Flat%20Flame%20Bulletin.pdf)). A lid washed by flame would radiate strongly onto the top shelf. Radiation travels in straight lines, though, so the top shelf shades everything below it, and the lower shelves depend on hot gas pulled down through the setting. That leads back to the floor flue.

A single central flat-flame burner is the off-the-shelf way to "spread the flame around the lid". The 4832 throws a disc-shaped, tangential flame that scrubs the surrounding refractory, with **less than 6 in of forward travel**. It is "sealed-in, nozzle mixing" and "can not flash back". On natural gas its smallest size covers about **58,500–234,000 Btu/h (17–69 kW)** as combustion-air pressure rises from 1 to 16 osi, and the next size about 88,500–354,000 Btu/h ([Fives 4832](https://webasset.fivesgroup.com/Energy_Combustion/Products_-_NA/Flat_Flame/4832_bul-North%20American%20Flat%20Flame%20Bulletin.pdf)). That fits the roughly 320,000 Btu/h one builder quotes for his cone 10 kiln ([Ceramic Arts Daily](https://community.ceramicartsdaily.org/topic/3808-building-a-cone-10-outdoor-gassalt-kiln/)). The costs are three. It needs a proper blower: "12 osi or more" at high fire, about 21 in w.c., against the 1–8 in w.c. typical of forge ribbon-burner blowers. Propane has to be specified to the factory. And it runs only within the excess-air limits above. Bloom's HTR burners also lie flat against a roof, start below 70,000 Btu/h (12 kW) and list propane among their fuels ([Bloom](https://www.bloomeng.com/burners-combustion-systems/)). The result is a radiant disc centred on the lid, not a ring round the edge, and whether it would heat the outer stacks of a round kiln evenly is untested.

## No catalogue burner bends into an arc, and no burner metal survives the hot face

Pine Ridge Burner is the only confirmed commercial maker of castable ribbon burners, and its construction is the model for holding many small flames. A 12-gauge steel shell carries a 1/4 in steel baffle welded solid. Alloy-steel metering orifices press-fitted into the baffle reach up into a 5 in cast block and feed "a honeycomb of larger delivery orifices". The block is rated to **3100 °F (1704 °C)** ([Pine Ridge](https://www.pineridgeburner.com/)). The GH series wants 7 in w.c. or more from its blower; the LP series runs on 1–5 in w.c. and suits "a small glory hole or metal forge". Pine Ridge publishes no Btu rating, port size, turndown or flashback claim, and kilns are not among the uses it lists ([Pine Ridge](https://www.pineridgeburner.com/)).

DIY practice comes from John Emmerling's published design, Frosty's naturally aspirated ribbon burner (NARB) and many forum builds. These use ports of roughly 1/8–3/8 in, cast round crayons or straws through a 2–5 in castable block. Builders work to about **13 parts solid face to 1 part port area** and tune by plugging ports until burn-back stops ([IForgeIron 61583](https://www.iforgeiron.com/topic/61583-looking-for-ribbon-burner-recommendations-on-size-and-placement/); [IForgeIron 71342](https://www.iforgeiron.com/topic/71342-some-narb-experimentation/); [IForgeIron 62061](https://www.iforgeiron.com/topic/62061-calculating-number-of-holes-for-ribbon-burner/)). Selas's industrial ribbon burners are cast iron, straight, 2–36 in long, and accept no more than 1-1/4 in w.c. of premix ([Selas](https://www.selas.com/product/industrial-burners/ribbon-burners/ribbon-burner/erb-utility-burner/)). **No source documents a curved or arc-shaped ribbon burner.** Castable can be poured to any plan, so two semicircles are easy to make. What is untested is spreading the mixture evenly along several feet of curved plenum. The forum fixes for uneven feed (perforated distributor pipes, inlets turned 90°, taller plenums) were all worked out on straight plenums of 3–12 in ([IForgeIron 61583](https://www.iforgeiron.com/topic/61583-looking-for-ribbon-burner-recommendations-on-size-and-placement/); [KnifeDogs](https://www.knifedogs.com/threads/ribbon-burner-build-for-new-forge.48423/)).

Metal ring and line burners make arcs easily, but all of them keep their bodies in cool air. Cast-iron boiling rings run about 17,000–19,000 Btu/h on propane ([BES](https://www.bes.co.uk/cast-iron-boiling-ring-double-burner-8862/)). A 20-tip cast-iron jet burner is listed at 110,000 Btu/h ([desertcart](https://www.desertcart.tw/products/274325415-20-tips-jet-burner)). Stainless fire-pit rings come in 6–36 in diameters ([Starfire Direct](https://starfiredirect.com/products/24-stainless-steel-fire-pit-ring-by-athena-fire-glass)). Eclipse's AH-MA line burner bolts cast-iron or aluminium sections into straight, tee and cross shapes, for upstream air up to 450 °F ([Honeywell Eclipse](https://process.honeywell.com/us/en/products/thermal-solutions/burners-and-heat-exchangers/line-and-duct-burners/eclipse-ah-ma-burner)). A Maxon line burner is limited to 1,000 °F downstream ([SurplusRecord](https://surplusrecord.com/listing/1-mm-btu-maxon-np2-le-airflo-in-duct-direct-fired-line-burner-natural-gas-propane-butane-301-turndown-1700-3000-fpm-air-velocity-2019-1133309/)). An arc built from line burners would need a custom casting or a polygon of short straight sections.

The geometry itself is old. Feinberg's 1951 house furnace feeds radial Bunsen burners from "a circular gas manifold coaxial with" the chamber, forming "substantially continuous concentric flame rings" ([US2567013](https://patents.google.com/patent/US2567013)). A ring burner for flame-seaming glass sheets varies port size and spacing along its straight and curved sections, which is the technique an arc manifold would need to even out its flame ([EP0479155](https://data.epo.org/publication-server/rest/v1.2/patents/EP0479155NWA2/document.html)). For drilled pipe, Allied Kenco's rule is that the holes should add up to at least 80% of the pipe's bore, with the first hole about 4 in from the venturi and holes no more than 1/2 in apart so the flame carries from one to the next ([Allied Kenco](https://www.alliedkenco.com/pdf/Build%20A%20Pipe%20Burner.pdf)). Its own example, 32 holes of 1/8 in in 3/4 in pipe, comes to only about 74% of a Schedule 40 bore (0.824 in ID); about 35 holes meet the 80% rule. Propane also needs a smaller gas orifice than natural gas, and yellow, candle-like flame tips mean too little primary air ([Hones](https://charlesahones.com/wp-content/uploads/2019/08/Basic-Venturi-Need-4-Page-8-2019.pdf)).

The cast manifold cannot sit at the hot face. At cone 6–10 the inside of the lid runs near **1220–1300 °C**. Gray cast iron oxidises and grows sharply above 700 °C ([steel-foundry.com](https://www.steel-foundry.com/blog/gray-cast-iron-performance-service-properties)), and ASTM A319 gray iron for high-temperature service tops out near 760 °C ([ASTM A319](https://softbank.iust.ac.ir/Railway/Codes/ASTM-standard/ASTM%202004%20Volume%2001.02%20Ferrous%20Castings_%20Ferroalloys/A%20319%20_%2071%20R01%20%20;QTMXOQ__.pdf)). The Nickel Institute rates Type 310 stainless at 1150 °C in continuous service but **1035 °C intermittent**. A kiln that is fired and cooled every cycle has to use the intermittent figure ([Nickel Institute](https://nickelinstitute.org/media/1699/high_temperaturecharacteristicsofstainlesssteel_9004_.pdf)). Inconel 601 reaches roughly 1205–1250 °C ([High Performance Alloys](https://www.hpalloys.com/Alloys/601)), which is at or below cone 6.

Shinagawa learned this in a top-fired tunnel kiln. Its refractory-steel burner tips "wear from oxidation when kiln temperature exceeds about 1300° C", glowed red and clogged with carbon. Water cooling wasted heat and risked an explosion if it leaked, so Shinagawa switched to silicon nitride or zirconia air nozzles ([US4240788A](https://patents.google.com/patent/US4240788A/en)). The rule that follows is industry's: metal stays behind the insulation, cooled by the incoming gas or air, and only refractory or ceramic ports reach the hot face, like Fives' tiles of 3000–3200 °F castable ([Fives 4832](https://webasset.fivesgroup.com/Energy_Combustion/Products_-_NA/Flat_Flame/4832_bul-North%20American%20Flat%20Flame%20Bulletin.pdf)). How hot the manifold side of a given lid runs has not been published for this case; it is a heat-loss calculation.

Whether the manifold carries premixed gas and air or raw gas is the most consequential choice. A premix manifold feeding many ports is a large ribbon burner. Ribbon burners burn back when "your flame front exceeds the velocity of the gas exiting the hole". When they are turned down, heat "creeps back into the burner head towards the plenum", and the mixture igniting in the plenum "will trash the burner quite quickly" ([IForgeIron 66730](https://www.iforgeiron.com/topic/66730-ribbon-burner-overheat/); [IForgeIron 61062](https://www.iforgeiron.com/topic/61062-ribbon-burner-backfire-question/)). One builder reported "a large VERY LOUD explosion in the forge after 45 minutes to an hour of use" ([IForgeIron 66730](https://www.iforgeiron.com/topic/66730-ribbon-burner-overheat/)). Builders also say ribbon burners turn down poorly because of the plenum ([IForgeIron 45919](https://www.iforgeiron.com/topic/45919-more-on-ribbon-burners/)). A pottery schedule spends hours at exactly the low flows where burn-back starts: candling, the slow early climb and reduction holds. A lid makes this worse, adding rising heat during the firing and a chimney effect after shutdown. Nozzle-mix burners remove the mechanism entirely: gas and air stay separate until the burner face, which is why Fives can say its burner "can not flash back".

The architecture these precedents point to is Holley's, though it has not been tested in a kiln. A raw-gas manifold sits outside the insulation and feeds several individual nozzle-mix power burners set in refractory blocks round the rim, angled tangentially, with ceramic tips at the hot face. For low fire, whole groups of burners are switched off rather than every port being turned down.

## A burner lid becomes a moving gas appliance no code anticipates

### The lid must carry weight, heat and a rigid manifold without cracking

Kiln lids already fail without burners in them. A brick lid bows into "a parabola/arc" as the hot face expands against the cold face, which stresses its banding ([CAD 3278](https://community.ceramicartsdaily.org/topic/3278-electric-kiln-lid-failure/)). Brick lids are heavy enough to overload hinges and prompt counterweights ([CAD 19651](https://community.ceramicartsdaily.org/topic/19651-rebuilding-kiln-lid/); [CAD 6663](https://community.ceramicartsdaily.org/topic/6663-help-me-decide-what-to-do-about-a-cracked-kiln-lid/)), and propping a lid open at one point is "bad for the lid, and bad for the top rim" ([CAD 15811](https://community.ceramicartsdaily.org/topic/15811-propping-kiln-lid/)). Burner blocks and a cast manifold add weight, and they put a rigid metal part with its own expansion on top of a refractory slab that is trying to bow.

Industrial burner tiles "suffer from severe thermal shock caused by heavy switching between burning and extinguishing" and crack or peel ([US10527283](https://image-ppubs.uspto.gov/dirsearch-public/print/downloadPdf/10527283)). Once a burner block fails, "the failure will often rapidly spread" ([US12259131](https://image-ppubs.uspto.gov/dirsearch-public/print/downloadPdf/12259131)). Hot gas leaking round a tile's expansion gap overheats the mounting plate, and Zeeco dries its tiles to 360 °C (680 °F) before start-up to avoid cracking ([Zeeco](https://www.zeeco.com/resources/news/taking-on-the-tiling-puzzle)). Aisin Takaoka built its second, burnerless lid precisely to protect burner life ([US10598437](https://patents.google.com/patent/US10598437)). The implications are a manifold supported separately from the refractory, with a flexible mount at each burner, and a counterbalanced or powered lift that raises the lid evenly. Rohde lifts its heavy electric hood kilns hydraulically on a guide rail ([Rohde HE 330](https://www.rohde.eu/en/arts-and-crafts/products/hood-kilns/he-330)), and counterbalanced hinges are sold for heavy kiln lids ([Weber Knapp](https://blog.weberknapp.com/motion-control-solutions-for-ceramic-studios)).

Insulation decides how hot everything on top of the lid runs, and the data are thin. One unverified archive account reports a 3 in soft-brick wall at about 2195 °F giving a bare steel skin near 1150 °F. Adding 1 in of fibre brought the steel down to about 750 °F and the outer surface to about 200 °F ([BC archive](https://bchdp-i2.arcabc.ca/_flysystem/repo-service/2025-06/113177-Extracted%20Text.txt)). Industrial cone 10 kilns use about 8 in of fibre on walls and ceiling, with refractories derated about 150 °F below their rating ([Ceramic Industry](https://www.ceramicindustry.com/articles/87858-pottery-production-practices-controlled-firings-kiln-design-matters/)). No measured skin temperature for a cone 10 lid was found.

### Gas reaches the lid through hose rated to only 60 °C

Standard LP-gas hose under UL 21 is rated from **−40 °C to 60 °C (140 °F)** ([GlobalSpec UL 21](https://standards.globalspec.com/std/14551783/ul-21)), far below the skin temperatures above. Forge users with top-mounted burners say "the gas hose gets a lot more hot than I am comfortable with", and some switch to copper line because it "won't burn like rubber hose" ([IForgeIron 67604](https://www.iforgeiron.com/topic/67604-concept-horizontal-front-mount-burner/); [IForgeIron 69072](https://www.iforgeiron.com/topic/69072-cooling-forge-chimney-effect/)). Connectors for appliances "mounted on casters or otherwise subject to movement" fall under ANSI Z21.69/CSA 6.16. Vendor literature says the standard requires a restraint to keep strain off the connector and that these connectors are rated for about 1/2 psi indoors; neither point has been checked against the standard itself ([ANSI](https://webstore.ansi.org/standards/csa/ansiz21692015csa16)). If that holds, the regulator has to sit upstream of any moving joint.

Holley's ladle cover is the industrial template for a moving burner. The needle valve, steel flexible hose, gauge and inspirator ride on a boom, the electrical controls sit on the boom, and the exhaust stacks reach the plant flue through flexible tubes ([US4014532A](https://patents.google.com/patent/US4014532A/en)). Most of industry avoids the problem by moving the ware rather than the burners. Hendryx's shuttle kiln and NUTEC Bickley's elevator kilns keep the burners on the fixed structure and move the car ([US3854865A](https://patents.google.com/patent/US3854865A/en); [NUTEC Bickley](https://www.nutecbickley.com/what-we-do/industrial-kilns-for-ceramics/elevator-kilns)), and no top-hat or bell kiln with burners on its lifting hood was found. A hose that catches fire is hard to stop. One user watched flame come from a burned-through regulator hose while the tank's excess-flow device failed to shut it, and closed the valve by hand ([Smoking Meat Forums](https://www.smokingmeatforums.com/threads/propane-regulator-heater.105018/post-614489)). That is why forge users fit a quarter-turn ball valve on the main line ([IForgeIron 69072](https://www.iforgeiron.com/topic/69072-cooling-forge-chimney-effect/)).

### Flame supervision must shut the gas before the lid can lift

NFPA 86 is the US standard for fuel-fired ovens and furnaces. It exempts only *listed* equipment of 150,000 Btu/h (44 kW) or less, so a homebuilt kiln of any size falls within it ([GlobalSpec NFPA 86](https://standards.globalspec.com/std/14562808/86)). A combustion-equipment maker told the American Ceramic Society that "ceramic kilns fall within the scope of NFPA 86" ([Lukacs](https://ceramics.org/wp-content/uploads/2009/07/kilnsincompliance_lukacs.pdf)), and Seattle's fire code adopts NFPA 86 for industrial ovens and furnaces ([Seattle Fire Code](https://seattle.gov/documents/departments/sdci/codes/seattlefirecode/2012seattlefirechapter30.pdf)).

As reported secondhand, NFPA 86 requires the flame safeguard to close the gas valves within **4 seconds** of losing the flame, and requires flame supervision whenever the chamber is below **1400 °F (760 °C)** ([Process Heating](https://digital.bnpmedia.com/article/Combustion+Control/3659189/658227/article.html)). It relaxes the repeat purge for multi-burner chambers that are proven hot, or that have two safety valves on each burner ([Intertek](https://cdn.intertek.com/www-intertek-com/dms-legacy/NFPA-86-Rev-5-24-2018-ED-5-24-2020.pdf)). A thermocouple-and-pilot valve, by contrast, takes "a minute or so" to close after the flame goes out ([US5718256](https://patents.google.com/patent/US5718256)). UV scanners respond quickly, but Fireye limits the scanner body to about 200 °F and wants the sight pipe no more than halfway through the refractory ([Fireye SC-107](https://www.fireye.com/Documents/SC-107.pdf)). Those limits are hard to meet on a lid top that runs hotter than 200 °F. Potters recommend flame-failure shut-off because a burner that blows out unattended before the kiln is red hot can lead to an explosion ([Dan Palmer](https://danpalmerceramics.com/2024/05/15/building-and-rebuilding-a-better-gas-kiln/)). A 1986 explosion at an Arizona curing kiln, which killed a worker, was blamed on faulty burner operation that let gas build up ([OSHA](https://www.osha.gov/ords/imis/accidentsearch.accident_detail?id=978536)).

UL 795 covers gas appliances above 400,000 Btu/h, and an older edition excluded process equipment ([ANSI UL 795](https://webstore.ansi.org/standards/ul/ul795ed2024)). In Canada, field approval runs through CSA B149.3, and Saskatchewan began applying the 2025 edition on 1 April 2026 ([TSASK](https://api.tsask.ca/wp-content/uploads/TSASK-CSA-B149.3-25-Interpretations-final-draft.pdf)). **No standard was found that addresses burners on a lid, door or hood that moves**, and no clause on lid-open interlocks for fuel-fired kilns. The precedents imply a minimum. A lid-position switch belongs in the safeguard's interlock chain, so the gas closes before the lid can lift. The chamber needs a fresh purge before relighting, unless it is proven above 1400 °F. All the clause numbers above are secondhand and must be checked against the edition the local authority has adopted.

## Conclusion

The user's idea is new only as a combination. Rim burners firing tangentially from a removable refractory cover (Holley, 1977), overhead burners driving gas down through a stacked setting to a floor exhaust (Hendryx, 1974), and multi-port refractory burner faces (Pine Ridge) each exist and work. Nobody has put them together in a periodic pottery kiln, and nothing found rules it out. The evidence moves the design question away from the shape of the arcs, which matters less than forge lore suggests, and toward the exhaust path and the burner type. A lid-fired kiln either works as a downdraft with sealed power burners or works as an updraft with a short circuit. The precedents also say something about proportions. Every successful roof-fired process found heats a shallow, open or flat load: bricks set two high, a glass melt, a steel slab, glass on one fusing shelf. That makes the concept a better fit for a wide, low chamber for glass, tile or single-layer work than for a tall stoneware stack. An elevator or front-loading kiln with a fixed burner crown would get the top-down heat without putting a gas appliance on a moving part, which is the route industry chose.

For kilndesigner, none of this is engineering data yet. There is no cone map, thermocouple survey or failure record for any lid-fired studio kiln. Under ADR 0009 the engine should refuse to rate a lid-burner layout rather than stretch floor-fired rules to cover it. The metal limits (1035 °C intermittent for 310 stainless, about 760 °C for heat-resisting gray iron) and the 60 °C hose rating are citable grounds for that refusal, once they are moved into `docs/research/` with their sources. One useful number is within the engine's reach: the existing heat-loss model can give the temperature at the lid's cold face, and that figure decides whether a manifold there needs stainless, a nickel alloy or simply more insulation. The cheapest next evidence would be a test firing of a small lid-fired chamber with a floor flue and cone packs on every shelf.
