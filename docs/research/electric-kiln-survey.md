# Survey: how round electric kilns are built in brick

Retrieved 2026-10-30. Purpose: learn how commercial faceted ("round") electric kilns size their
walls, so the engine's ring layout can be checked against real kilns.

sheet's 28.00 fits 3 in brick. Either the sheet's KM-1227 is built of 3 in brick, or "Opening

**Resolved by the user (2026-10-30):** the kiln is 3 in brick, a KM-1227-3. Skutt's names read

## Derived: sides, brick thickness and inside diameter

**Derived.** Count check: KM-1227 has 60 + 6 + 5 = 71 wall bricks (snippet). A 27 in wall of 4½ in
courses is 6 courses; 6 x 12 sides = 72. So each course appears to be one brick per facet, laid
flat on its 4½ in face, 9 in long around the ring, 4½ in tall, and t (2½ or 3 in) through the wall.
One brick short of 72 is unexplained (perhaps a part counted elsewhere).

Model: a regular N-gon whose **outside** face per facet is 9 in (the full brick length; the brick
ends are cut to the miter angle). Outer apothem a_o = 4.5 / tan(π/N); inner apothem a_i = a_o − t.
Inside flat-to-flat = 2·a_i; inside corner-to-corner = 2·a_i / cos(π/N).

| Model | N | t (in) | Flat-to-flat | Corner-to-corner | Mean | Published | Off (mean) |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| KM-818 | 8 | 2.5 | 16.73 | 18.11 | 17.42 | 17.5 | −0.08 |
| KM-818-3 | 8 | 3 | 15.73 | 17.02 | 16.37 | 16.5 | −0.13 |
| KM-1027 | 10 | 2.5 | 22.70 | 23.87 | 23.28 | 23.375 (23) | −0.09 |
| KM-1027-3 | 10 | 3 | 21.70 | 22.82 | 22.26 | 22 | +0.26 |
| L&L e23T-3 | 10 | 3 | 21.70 | 22.82 | 22.26 | 22.30 | −0.04 |
| KM-1227-3 | 12 | 3 | 27.59 | 28.56 | 28.07 | 28.125 (28) | −0.05 |

Every published diameter lies between the inner flat-to-flat and corner-to-corner of this model,
and the mean of the two matches within about 0.1 in (0.26 in for the dealer-rounded "22").

Other conventions tested and rejected:

- 9 in on the **inside** face: KM-1227-3 would be 33.6 in flat-to-flat, 5 in too big.
- 9/sin(π/N) − 2t (outer corner circle less two walls): KM-1027 gives 24.1 in, KM-1227-3 28.8 in;
  0.7 to 0.8 in too big.

The 4½ in dimension is the course height (matches L&L's "9" high sections" = two courses), not the
wall thickness.

## What this means for the engine

1. Commercial round electric kilns use **2½ in or 3 in** walls (Skutt "2.5"" standard, "-3" models
   3 in; L&L "3" K23"), not 4½ in. Evidence: Clay King pages (Verified), dealer snippets.
2. One brick per facet per course: the **9 in face is the facet length on the outside**, 4½ in is
   the course height, t is the wall. Evidence: KM-1227 count 71 ≈ 6 x 12; KM-818 "8 bricks around".
3. Inside diameter fits **mean of inner flat-to-flat and corner-to-corner** with
   a_i = 4.5/tan(π/N) − t, within ~0.1 in on five models (derived). Inner flat-to-flat is the
   conservative "fits a round shelf" size.
4. Sides used: 8 (≈17½ in), 10 (≈22–23 in), 12 (≈28 in). Larger kilns add facets rather than use
   longer bricks.
5. Brick grade for Skutt, mortar vs dry stack, course bond and groove spacing were **not found**;
   do not assume them. A Skutt spec sheet or a parts diagram readable as text is the next source to
   get.
