# Kiln fuelwood by region: the wood each reference kiln burned, and how wet it was

Read 2026-11-10, for round A1 (`docs/planning/11-10-26.md`): which wood each wood kiln burned and its
moisture. Composition and heating value are sourced separately. Method as `wood-fuel.md` §6: the
book's words first (Olsen, *The Kiln Book*, 4th ed., as `olsen-kilns.md` records them), then a
source about kilns of that place, then the region's woods from a forestry or government source,
marked as such. Quotes are as the page gave them.

Marks: **R** Read (on the fetched page, a PDF's text layer, or a page image); **S** Snippet (only in
a search result or a summarising tool's paraphrase that could not be checked); **D** Derived
(arithmetic shown); **M** from memory (not to be relied on); **NF** not found. "Book" = Olsen, as in
`olsen-kilns.md`. A wood "by analogy" is the practice of the same tradition elsewhere, not a source
for that kiln. Station "kinds" (same city, same region) are general geography (M).

## 1. The woods

### Japan: pine, and red pine where named

**[K1] Kyoto City, 「元藤平陶芸登り窯の歴史的価値等調査研究 報告書」** (report on the former Fujihira
pottery's climbing kiln, Gojōzaka), 平成27年3月 (March 2015).
<https://www.city.kyoto.lg.jp/kyoiku/cmsfiles/contents/0000182/182621/honnpenn.pdf> (text layer
extracted, R).
- p.17 (Maezaki Shinya, on the Gojōzaka shared kilns): the firing crew came with 「松割木を割る専門の
  職人」 (men whose trade was splitting the pine); 「燃料の松割木の準備がおわれば、窯焚き開始である。」
  (once the fuel, split pine, is ready, the firing starts). 「昭和46 年（1971）… 京都府公害防止条例の
  施行により京都市内の登り窯に排出規制が課された。」 The cooperative's shared kiln, refitted against
  smoke in 1973, was stopped in spring 1982 after a fire in 1980.
- p.33: the threat to Kyoto's climbing kilns began before pollution, 「燃料となる薪不足の問題から昭和20
  年代の段階で既におこっていた」 (a firewood shortage, already in the 1945–54 decade).
- pp.50–51, the Fujihira kiln's 1969 smoke-facility filing: 「燃料 種類 松薪」 (fuel: pine firewood);
  「1 日の使用量 1, 500 束/1 回」 (1,500 bundles a firing); 「P.M.6 時～2 回目P.M4 時 46 時間/回」; the
  report's reading: 「焼成時間は46 時間… 使用する松薪は1500 束であるという」, and 「おおよその概算で
  あろう」 (probably a rough figure). The kiln had stood idle since March 1968. The same filing gives
  9 chambers (one a waste chamber), a fire-mouth grate area 「2.4 ㎡」, flues 「0.675 ㎡×8＝5.4 ㎡」 and a
  brick chimney 「60 ㎝2×15ｍ」 (for round A2).

Pine (松) is named; the species is not. Red pine is Japan's usual kiln pine by [K2]–[K5]; for Kyoto
that is analogy.

## 2. Moisture: outdoor equilibrium moisture content

**[S1] Simpson, W. T. (1998), "Equilibrium moisture content of wood in outdoor locations in the
United States and worldwide", Res. Note FPL-RN-0268, USDA Forest Service, Forest Products
Laboratory**, August 1998. <https://research.fs.usda.gov/download/treesearch/5913.pdf>. The tables
are image-only; every row below was read off the page images at high zoom (R), and the Hilo row
recomputes to `wood-fuel.md` §6's 14.0 % mean (check).
- Printed p.1: EMC of wood "exposed to the outdoor atmosphere but protected from direct precipitation
  or sun"; Table 2 has "262 locations in the United States, Puerto Rico, and Pacific Island
  territories", Table 3 "122 locations outside the United States".
- p.2: Table 2 mostly from NCDC (1997), 15 locations from Wallis (1977), "at least 30 years of
  observation"; Table 3's relative humidities "were derived from air and dew point temperatures (USA
  Today 1998; original data from NCDC)". Hysteresis "can be as much as 3% moisture content"; species
  with "a high extractive content" sit lower at high humidity.
- The equation (p.1, as corrected by the errata sheet, which adds the outer bracket): EMC = (1,800/W)
  [Kh/(1 − Kh) + (K₁Kh + 2K₁K₂K²h²)/(1 + K₁Kh + K₁K₂K²h²)], h = RH/100, EMC in %; for T in °C:
  W = 349 + 1.29T + 0.0135T²; K = 0.805 + 0.000736T − 0.00000273T²; K₁ = 6.27 − 0.00938T − 0.000303T²;
  K₂ = 1.91 + 0.0407T − 0.000293T² (Hailwood–Horrobin; Simpson 1973). Check (D): T = 20 °C, h = 0.65
  gives W = 380.2, K = 0.8186, K₁ = 5.961, K₂ = 2.607, EMC = 4.734 × 2.534 = 12.0 %.

**What EMC means here.** It is the floor that wood stored under cover approaches, not the moisture
of wood as fired; uncovered, unsplit or freshly cut wood stays wetter (`wood-fuel.md` §6).

**Seasoning in these traditions.** Korea: pine "dried for a year" [G1]. Japan: "well-dried" split
red pine [K5]; how long, NF. Denmark: split wood dried "at least one, preferably two summers", from
over 75 % to 15–30 % of dry weight [E5]. Alberta: "at least one summer", 15–20 % [C1]. New Hampshire:
6–8 months, "seasoned around 30%" [N1]. None of these measures kiln wood as fired: NF.

## 3. Bundles and slabs

- Kyoto: 1,500 bundles of pine for one 46 h firing of the Fujihira kiln, a figure the report calls
  rough [K1]. The bundle's size and weight aren't given.
- Bizen: a "set" is five sticks, each "10cm in diameter, 1m in length", and one kiln burns 1,000
  sets in 10 days [K4]. D, taking each stick as a full cylinder (an upper bound for split wood):
  π/4 × 0.10² × 1.0 = 0.00785 m³ a stick; 0.0393 m³ a set; 39.3 m³ of solid wood a firing.
- Olsen's bundles (book): Tamba, "15 x 30in" (p.87), round, 15 in across and 30 in long (the
  owner, 2026-11-18; first read here as 15 × 15 × 30 in from the metric's "38 x 38 x 76cm"):
  π/4 × 15² × 30 = 5,301 in³ = 3.07 ft³ = 0.0869 m³ of bundle; about 200 a firing = 614 ft³,
  4.8 cords of stacked volume (D). Suzuki, "15 in across × 18 in": 0.052 m³ if round, 0.066 m³ if
  square (D). Applying a cord's 65–90 ft³ of solid wood per 128 ft³ ([W1]) to a Tamba bundle gives
  0.044–0.061 m³ of wood, but that packing is an assumption, not a source for bundles.
- Weight of a Japanese kiln-wood bundle: NF. Slab wood's weight or solid content per volume: NF.
  The red pine's density, which would turn [K4]'s volume into a mass, wasn't found in a source read.
