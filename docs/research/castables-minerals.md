KMC [M1] also says coarse grains give more *apparent* linear expansion because they push on their
neighbours, while fines fill voids. Its particle-size table is an image and was not read.

pre-cast −50 or −100 mesh 4–8 %; insulating firebrick coarser sizes 10–20 %; mortars 20–30 %.

Note: 0.45 % linear would be about 1.35 % volume **(derived, 3 × linear)**, which conflicts
with the "0.4 %" and "0.5 %" volume figures. Those may really be linear. Not settled, and no
primary handbook (Kingery, Hamer, Rhodes, Cardew, Chesters) was read. **NOT FOUND** at primary level.

### 2.1 Quartz → cristobalite in fired clay

- Conversion depends on temperature, quartz fineness (finer converts more) and soak time; high-quartz,
  low-flux bodies cooled slowly through 1500–800 °C form the most [M5] (Verified, secondary).
- Bodies with a strong glass phase form little or none; clusters of quartz can still form it
  [M6] (Excerpt, Ceramics Monthly).
- In a brick study, cristobalite formed from the silica-rich matrix at about 1300 °C, after mullite
  had formed above 1000 °C [M7] (Excerpt).
- Repeated firings: the only evidence found is for **silica brick**, not fireclay. NBS Technologic
  Paper T124: after the first burn about two-thirds cristobalite; after 10 burns largely tridymite;
  after 50 burns almost all tridymite [M8] (Excerpt).

**NOT FOUND:** a source saying how fast free quartz in a fireclay castable becomes cristobalite
over repeated firings at cone 6–10. Sources: 3 for the factors, 1 for the temperature, 0 for fireclay
refiring.

## 4. Mulcoa 70

| Property | Value | Status | Source |
|---|---|---|---|
| Bulk density (packed grain) | 2.88 g/cm³ | Verified (secondary) | [S31] |
| Apparent porosity | 3.5 % | Verified (secondary) | [S31] |
| Density on SDS | "2 – 3 g/cm³" | Verified | [S58] |
| Chemistry | Al₂O₃ 68.8, SiO₂ 26.8, TiO₂ 2.8, Fe₂O₃ 1.2 %; 87 % mullite; PCE 39 | Verified (secondary) | [S31] |
| LKAB chemistry table | lists Mulcoa 45/60/70 chemistry and grades, no density | Excerpt | [M11] |
| True (skeletal) specific gravity | NOT FOUND | | |

A grain's apparent (skeletal) density ≈ bulk ÷ (1 − apparent porosity) = 2.88 ÷ 0.965 = **2.98 g/cm³
(derived)**, if 2.88 is the grain bulk density per ASTM C357 and not a packed-powder density.
Digitalfire's label is ambiguous. Closed pores are not counted, so true density is higher.
Sources: 1 secondary for each number.

## 5. Dry-out schedules

| Maker / source | Schedule | Thickness rule | Status | Source |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| HWI Mizzou (via High Temp Tools) | 75 °F/h; holds at 250, 500, 1000 °F | ½ h per inch, **"single layer 9 in or less"** | Verified (secondary) | [S41] |
| HWI, J. Bogan, Ceramic Industry 2018 | holds at 302 °F (150 °C), 572 °F (300 °C), 1049 °F (565 °C), after Hipps & Brown; no rates or durations | **"the thickest section should be used to establish the schedule"**; length depends on material, thickness, "number of components in the lining" | Verified | [M12] |
| AGC Ceramics | 50 °C/h to 200 °C, then hold | "10 mm thickness corresponds to 1 hour" (one product class), "20 mm … 1 hour" (another) = ~2.5 or ~1.3 h/in **(derived)** | Excerpt (PDF 404) | [S56] [M13] |
| Unnamed supplier guide (Forno Bravo copy) | hold 100–150 °C 8–12 h by thickness; 500–600 °C 1 h per 25 mm | "+1 h per step for each 25 mm above 200 mm" | Excerpt (docx not readable) | [M14] |
| Morgan Kao Tuff RFT | up to 100 °F/h, no holds | | Excerpt | [M15] |
| Morgan Morflo 160, Kaocrete D | cure 24 h damp; heat slowly first time; schedules on request | none | Excerpt | [M15] |
| Refratechnik | hold operating temperature 12–24 h | | Excerpt | [M15] |
| Allied Mineral, Plibrico, Vesuvius, Kast-O-Lite own sheet | NOT FOUND | | | |

**Multiple layers:** no maker found states a rule for summing layers. HWI's guidance is to use the
thickest section and to count the number of lining components; the Mizzou rule is limited to one layer
of ≤ 9 in. So the engine should not assume holds scale with total lining thickness; it should
refuse or flag multi-layer or > 9 in linings (ADR 0009) until a maker rule is found.
Sources: 2 primary-ish (HWI), 3 Excerpts.

## 6. Drying heat

| Item | Value | Status | Source |
|---|---|---|---|
| Water cp, 1 atm | 4.1841 J/g·K at 20 °C; 4.1794 at 40; 4.1968 at 80; 4.2156 at 99.97 °C | Verified | [M16] |
| Water enthalpy, 1 atm | 84.007 kJ/kg at 20 °C; 419.06 kJ/kg saturated liquid at 99.97 °C | Verified | [M16] |
| Heat 20 → 100 °C | 419.06 − 84.01 = **335.1 kJ/kg (derived)** | | [M16] |
| Then evaporate at 100 °C | 2256.4 kJ/kg (NIST) | Verified | [S15] |
| Kaolinite dehydroxylation ΔH | ~148–159 kJ/mol from a kinetic/thermodynamic study | Excerpt | [M17] |
| as kJ/kg kaolinite | 148–159 ÷ 0.2582 kg/mol = **573–616 kJ/kg (derived)**, M(Al₂Si₂O₅(OH)₄) = 258.16 g/mol | | [M17] |
| Endotherm peak | 500–560 °C, shifts with particle size, ordering and heating rate | Excerpt | [M18] |
| Calorimetric value (Vaughan 1955; LRL report on four kaolins) | NOT FOUND (PDF unreadable here) | | [M19] |

The ΔH comes from kinetic modelling, not calorimetry, and has only one source. Use it only as a
flagged estimate until Vaughan 1955 or the LRL report is read.

## 7. Shrinkage of fireclay castables and clays

| Material | Value | Status | Source |
|---|---|---|---|
| Versaflow 45 Plus (fireclay, low cement) | PLC −0.2 % after 1500 °F; −0.5 % after 2700 °F | Excerpt (Scribd copy) | [M20] |
| Firelite 2500 G (Morgan, insulating gun mix) | PLC −0.3 % after 5 h at 815 °C | Excerpt | [M20] |
| Unnamed castable | −0.4 to −0.2 % after 5 h at 1200 °C | Excerpt | [M20] |
| Hawthorn Bond clay | ~12 % total at cone 10; drying 4.5 %, firing ~8 % at cone 11, 7 % at cone 6 (1997 sample) | Verified (secondary) | [M9] |
| Lincoln fireclay | drying 6.0 % | Verified (secondary) | [S29] |
| Curing time affects PLC after firing to 816 °C | API TR 983 | Excerpt | [M20] |

Dense commercial castables shrink well under 1 % linear; raw fireclay shrinks 10 % or more, so a
castable made mostly of raw clay (as in the notes) will sit near the clay figures, not the castable
ones. This is an inference, not a measurement. Every sheet says its data are not for specification.
