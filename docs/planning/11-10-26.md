# Round of 2026-11-10: engine v2's heat model across the reference kilns

Inputs: ADR 0017 (its decisions, and the bar in decision 17), `11-09-26.md` §H (v2's statics: 51 of
the 53 reference kilns stand cold and hot) and §I (v2's heat model: built, tried on three kilns),
`docs/engine-v2/problem-set.md`, `docs/research/thermal-software.md` and
`thermal-combinations.md`. Branch `v2-heat`.

## The rules for this round (the owner)

- **The reference kilns are working examples** (2026-11-10): "the kilns in the reference material
  all worked for the people that built them for the purpose they were built for. Those are the
  starting point, and the model has to let them stand as working examples. Formulas then go to get
  the right info for the engine, and existing modules are used in the right places in combo with
  the right bridges." Where v2 can't let a kiln stand or reach its documented firing, the model or
  the design file's inputs are at fault: the fix is sourced physics or the book's own figures,
  never a parameter fitted to the kiln, never a flag on it.
- **The must-fail cases yield to the reference kilns** (2026-11-10): "if the n1-n6 are in conflict
  with the reference kilns, those need to change." A negative case from the literature that would
  condemn a reference kiln as built is restated (its limit or its premise), never the kiln.
- "Anything useful is allowed if accurate and can run on a desktop, although like for like
  functionality is better faster." A bridge module is cheaper than a whole science module.
- First principles, nothing fitted; a quantity no source gives is carried as a stated band
  (ADR 0017 decisions 3, 4).

## Where it stands

v2's heat model (`core/src/kilndesigner/v2/heat/`) has the gas, the draft, rays with obstruction,
gas radiation (Bordbar 2014), walls, one zone and the firing on IDA, the ground, target times, zones
in series and burners at their supply pressure. It has been run on three kilns:
- Olsen's fast-fire and W15 reach their firings, but only as bounds: cone 10 at 0.40 h against the
  book's 3½ h, and 46 % of the wood's heat into walls and setting against 12–22 % measured on wood
  kilns. The losses and limits it lacks are named (§I): the fireboxes' and chimney's own brick,
  the fuel bed's burning rate, excess air through the stoking cycle, leaks.
- The 36 ft³ cross-draft is an open item on inputs the book doesn't print (§I question 1).

ADR 0017's bar (decision 17): the book's printed figures inside v2's bands; the must-fail cases
fail (as restated by the rule above); all 53 kilns as built stand.

### B1 as built: every chamber shape through the rays

**Several gas zones in one enclosure** (`rays.py`, `gasrad.py`). Each gas zone owns slabs of space
(`rays.Region`) that tile what the rays cross, so each ray's path splits exactly into its lengths in
each zone. The zone method's direct exchange areas, surface–gas and gas–gas included, then come from
the surface rays alone: rays leaving the surfaces uniformly with cosine directions sample the lines
through the enclosure with the kinematic measure, each chord from both ends (Derived, from the
definitions as Monnaemang 2015 eqs. 3.11–3.14 give them and the Blaschke–Petkantschin change of
variables). Tallied at each grey gas's k, closed by Larsen & Howell over the gas columns too; a
gas zone gains Σ g s (J − E) + Σ g g (E_w − E), so the enclosure conserves energy exactly
(`gasrad.ZonesExchange`). Checks: a cube as two halves (surface rows exact, gas rows within the
noise of 4kV, the halves' g s summing to one zone's, each half's volume from its own chords within
0.5 %); a sphere's halves against its closed form within 5e-3; energy to round-off; at one gas
temperature the two zones give what one does (1e-3).

**Every shape the engine draws** (`chamber.py`): box chambers under an arch, a sail dome, a catenary
(Ward's parabola), a flat roof or none; round chambers (the cylinder named "wall") under a dome, a
flat roof or open at the top ("open top"); chambers in a row, each its own gas zone, joined through
the flue holes in the walls between them (`run_parts.flue_rows`), the holes' sides "flues k" and
both faces of a wall between built in it; a single chamber cut into segments along its length;
crossing gas paths (Fireworks, Church: the two tubes' insides as one, the groin where their vaults
meet). The solid is split at the zones' cuts so no face straddles two zones; a run's slope turns the
whole solid without changing what any face sees, so the rays run on the run laid out straight; a bent
run is refused. Openings: `draft.exits` carved where the engine cuts them; a wall firebox's inlets in
its wall; an opening past its face's edge slid onto it at its size, and one with no flat face (a
catenary's side, a round wall, a dome) left out, each said. All 63 example files trace; Cauchy's
volume from the rays is within 0.7 % of the solid's on every one, and each gas zone's Hottel row
within 0.1 %.

Checked against closed forms: a round chamber's floor and roof as coaxial discs (Lienhard Table 10.3
case 3; `round-electric`) within 6e-3; Breda's dome as `radiation.covered` gives it over the
cylinder within 6e-3, its cap area π(a² + rise²) to 0.3 %; a sail dome's area and each wall's
segment over the springline as `shape.SailDome` gives them to 0.3 %; a catenary's vault the
parabola's length times the depth to 0.2 %; the Fireworks crossing's volume against
`groin.CrossingShape` to 0.5 %; the two-chamber kiln's zones each by Cauchy within 1 %.

Found and fixed on the way: (1) Embree works in float32, whose spacing grows with distance from the
origin, so 1 µm origin offsets were lost far along a long kiln and rays hit their own triangle:
Tamba's chambers 3–7 lost 4–9 % of their chords and Tobe 11.6 %; the offset now scales with the
scene (within 0.7 % after). (2) OCCT's 0.2 rad angular deflection left a dome's area 0.37 % short:
0.05 rad. (3) The closure now holds an entry the correction drives negative at zero (an active set)
before refusing.

The single-zone heat model (`kiln.heat_model`) still takes one gas zone in a box chamber; the rows,
tubes, round and crossing chambers reach it through B2–B3.

### B2, second half: the fireboxes and the chimney as zones (H13)

**The model** (`zone.py`, `firing.py`, `kiln.py`; `heat_model(..., path=True)`): well-stirred gas
zones in the gas's order, firebox, chamber, chimney, one enclosure (B1's rays), one DAE. The fuel
burns in the firebox zone; each zone keeps F·[H_in − H_out] of the stream (the air before the
burning zone, the products from it), less its gas's net radiation (the zone method over the zones)
and its convection; the grate is the fire's surface (black at the firebox's gas, inside the fire's
control volume, as an inlet flue was). Each zone's walls their own brick, transient: the
fireboxes' Smithfield (low-duty) and the floor's KX-99 over them as a wall between two zones, its
underside the fireboxes' roof; the chimney the engine's (one 4½ in layer of KX-99,
`hardware.CHIMNEY_BRICK`, Pitelka [S4]) from the exit flue's channel to its open top, convection in
it as flow in a duct (ht: the larger of Baehr–Stephan's developing laminar flow and Gnielinski's
turbulent, so it doesn't jump at transition). At max fire the draft's air is an unknown of its
own, its row the pressure balance round the path at each zone's temperature; in the other modes the
draft's most air is found after the firing by bisection, at the brick roughness band's both ends
(the air row, Newton-solved, failed where the products near room temperature are denser than air
and the draft reverses, and where a duct's friction factor jumps at transition). One zone burning
its fuel is the model as first built: the fast-fire's max fire to cone 10 in 0.395 h either way.

### B3 as built: the design's rows and tubes as zones in series

`heat_model(kiln, loads)` builds a row of chambers (`chamber.chambers`) or a single chamber cut into
segments (`segments=`) as gas zones of one enclosure from the engine's own cells: one load each
(test inputs: the book prints none), each wall between two chambers a wall with a face in each
(`common_wall.between_layers`), the flue holes' sides in it. `series.py` fires it in **stages**:
each chamber stoked in turn until its setting reaches its target, the fuel burning in that
chamber's zone, the air reaching it through the chambers before it (the stream is air there,
cooling their ware) and its gas preheating those after; each stage carries on from the last's
state, and its time is reported. The old two-enclosure links (`fire_series`) are gone: the one
enclosure gives the gas-to-gas exchange the links couldn't.

Runs, to show the path works (test inputs throughout: each chamber's setting its envelope 0.1 m in

### D2 as built (2026-11-10): dense brick's 25 psi sourced; Tobe hot

**The sources** (`refractory-bearing.md` §B6). ASTM C16 has one load, 25 psi, held 1½ h at its
schedules' temperatures. The makers of the library's three dense classes report their C16 results
at Schedule 3 (Alsey, rev. 2019, the PDFs read): super-duty Pilot D.P. and high-duty Jet D.P. 1.3 %,
medium-duty Smithfield 6.8 % subsidence at 2640 °F; KX-99 0.6 %. NBS's 1925 survey printed the load
itself, 25 psi at 1,350 °C for 1½ h, on 43 brands of fire-clay brick: 0.35–8.6 %, two failed. No
creep (C832) result for these classes, and no hot crushing strength, was found.

**The stand-in** (`v2/statics/sources.py`). C16_DENSE is now sourced on IFB's footing: the hot load
the class is tested and reported under, a load the brick carries hot, flagged as a stand-in for a
hot crushing strength no source gives. The analogy is gone from the code. One rule now serves both
stand-ins, since a load carried is a lower bound on strength: a section that stands with the
