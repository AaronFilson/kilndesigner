# Round of 2026-11-10: engine v2's heat model across the reference kilns

Inputs: ADR 0017 (its decisions, and the bar in decision 17), `11-09-26.md` §H (v2's statics: 51 of
the 53 reference kilns stand cold and hot) and §I (v2's heat model: built, tried on three kilns),
`docs/engine-v2/problem-set.md`, `docs/research/thermal-software.md` and
`thermal-combinations.md`. Branch `v2-heat`.

## The rules for this round (the owner)

- **The reference kilns are working examples** (2026-11-10): "the kilns in the reference material
  all worked for the people that built them for the purpose they were built for. Those are the
  starting point, and the model has to let them stand as working examples. Formulas then go to get
  the right info for the engine, and existing modules are used in the right places in combo with
  the right bridges." Where v2 can't let a kiln stand or reach its documented firing, the model or
  the design file's inputs are at fault: the fix is sourced physics or the book's own figures,
  never a parameter fitted to the kiln, never a flag on it.
- **The must-fail cases yield to the reference kilns** (2026-11-10): "if the n1-n6 are in conflict
  with the reference kilns, those need to change." A negative case from the literature that would
  condemn a reference kiln as built is restated (its limit or its premise), never the kiln.
- "Anything useful is allowed if accurate and can run on a desktop, although like for like
  functionality is better faster." A bridge module is cheaper than a whole science module.
- First principles, nothing fitted; a quantity no source gives is carried as a stated band
  (ADR 0017 decisions 3, 4).

## Where it stands

v2's heat model (`core/src/kilndesigner/v2/heat/`) has the gas, the draft, rays with obstruction,
gas radiation (Bordbar 2014), walls, one zone and the firing on IDA, the ground, target times, zones
in series and burners at their supply pressure. It has been run on three kilns:
- Olsen's fast-fire and W15 reach their firings, but only as bounds: cone 10 at 0.40 h against the
  book's 3½ h, and 46 % of the wood's heat into walls and setting against 12–22 % measured on wood
  kilns. The losses and limits it lacks are named (§I): the fireboxes' and chimney's own brick,
  the fuel bed's burning rate, excess air through the stoking cycle, leaks.
- The 36 ft³ cross-draft is an open item on inputs the book doesn't print (§I question 1).

ADR 0017's bar (decision 17): the book's printed figures inside v2's bands; the must-fail cases
fail (as restated by the rule above); all 53 kilns as built stand.

## A. Data the heat model needs (research: docs and dataset facts)

1. **Each kiln's own fuel** (decision 12): the wood species of each wood kiln and its composition,
   moisture and net heating value, as sourced bands; the oil kilns' oil; coal for the coal kilns,
   or the reason it stays refused.
2. **The firebox air intakes** (decision 9): what the reference material says about sizing
   ash-pit mouths, primary and secondary air and stoke holes, and each kiln's intake areas
   (printed, scaled or read off the drawings, by the precedence of `11-09-26.md` §A).
3. **The fuel bed**: grate burning rates for wood and coal from open sources, against the grates
   the kilns print (Olsen's 7 : 1, pp.76–77; coal ÷ 8, p.206).
4. **Rectangular-duct fittings** (Idelchik or an open equivalent) and **convection inside a
   kiln** (a sourced correlation to replace the laminar flat-plate stand-in).
5. **The luminous flame**: what open sources give for a wood or oil flame's soot, as the options
   for the owner's question (§I heat question 4).
6. **The kits' stacking space** (decision 14): the chamber each kit needs around its printed
   stacking volume (pp.169–170, p.173).

### A: progress (research agent, its own worktree; each topic committed as it was done)

- **A6, the kits** (`docs/research/kit-stacking-space.md`; dataset: each kit's chamber as
  assumed facts, research A6): the book sizes a kiln by its stacking space and builds the chamber
  round it, 4½ in of combustion space for 2½–3 in burner tips (6 in for a 4 in tip, p.171), 2½ in
  under the setter shelf (p.172), the updraft's arch on top of the cube (p.74). By those rules the
  kits need 1.4–2.0 × their stacking volume to the springline: 16 → 29–35 ft³, 24 → 41–47, 50 →
  77–85, 100 → 141–154 ft³ (the band is 4½–6 in of combustion space). The four kilns that print
  both run 1.15–1.9 (Kähler's 16 ft³ salt kiln: 30 ft³ to the arch line). Open: whether the 16's
  25 % extra height (p.170) is the chamber's or the setting's (under 1 ft³ either way).
- **A5, the luminous flame** (`docs/research/luminous-flame.md`): no kiln flame's soot, KL or
  luminous emissivity in any open source, in oxidation or reduction. Measured elsewhere: a pine-pellet
  flame 1.21 ppm at its peak (0.61 path-averaged, Yan 2021), stem wood in a gasifier 1.0 ppm at φ 0.5;
  an 800 kW heavy-oil furnace 0.04–0.3 ppm at λ 1.2 (Ishii 2005); Sato and Kunitomo's 1969 oil furnace
  7000 mg/Nm³ at λ 0.64 falling to 200–1450 at λ 1.0, and the soot's emissivity 0.03–2.9 × the gas's by
  fuel and λ, scale-free against IJmuiden. Every source finds soot rising steeply below λ ≈ 1. Models,
  sourced and open: RadLib's κ = 1817·f_v·T added to each grey gas (MIT; Bordbar's suggestion),
  Sadeghi 2021's soot WSGG (CC BY). Over 0.5 m at 1500 K soot alone is 0.01 at 0.01 ppm, 0.09–0.13 at
  0.1 ppm, 0.58–0.74 at 1 ppm: the band of f_v decides, not the model. The options for heat question 4
  are in the file: (i) leave it out and say so (the gas-only exchange is a lower bound), (ii) an f_v band
  through RadLib's form (wood 0–1.2 ppm, oil 0.01–0.3, to 0.7 fuel-rich, gas 0–0.07), (iii) a luminous
  emissivity band, (iv) soot tied to λ.

## B. The heat model's missing physics (engine)

1. **Every chamber shape through the rays**: round chambers and domes, tubes as segments, rows of
   chambers, crossing gas paths; `chamber.py` names faces beyond the box.
2. **The fireboxes and the chimney as zones** (H13), with their own brick. The fast-fire's two
   fireboxes laid as the book draws them, along the front and back walls in an S (p.140, p.204),
   their inlet flues on the floor at their printed size (§I heat question 5; the engine's layout
   follows the book by the precedence of `11-09-26.md` §A).
3. **The design's rows and tubes as zones in series** (`series.py` from the engine's cells and
   the flues between chambers): the multi-chamber stage times (Suzuki p.93) become runnable.
4. **λ from the air intakes and the draft** through the firing, reduction included (decision 9);
   **stoking within the draft's air and the fuel bed's rate** (H11), on A's data.
5. **Bands** (decision 17): each uncertain input's band carried to the results (emissivity class,
   the WSGG set, brick roughness, the fuel's moisture and heating value, a cord's weight, the
   load, λ), so a printed figure can be tested inside v2's band.
6. **Burners sized from the charts** (decision 13); **the kits resized** from their stacking space
   (decision 14); **elements** as face sources (electric-car-45; Olsen's W25, pp.258–260; Table
   9-6, p.257).

## C. Every reference kiln with a printed heat figure

The problem set's tests (§6: T1 reaches its cone, T2 time at a printed input, T3 fuel against
heat, T4 the printed log, T5 evenness) on every kiln that prints a figure: 12 with firing times, 7
with fuel per firing, 10 with fuel rates, 8 with cones, 19 with cone spreads, 6 with stage times.
Each is a test, with v2's band beside the book's figure. A kiln that doesn't stand is traced to its
inputs or the missing physics and kept as an open item with its question; nothing is fitted.
