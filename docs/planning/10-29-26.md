# Plan, 29 October 2026: Olsen's kilns as the ground truth

The user's request: review the engine against the reference material (Olsen, *The Kiln Book*,
4th ed.), add all of its knowledge, and make every kiln the book describes an example that the
engine builds **without an error**. The book's kilns are real and fired; the repo's own examples
were invented and are not to be trusted. A finding that fails a book kiln is a problem in the
engine, not the kiln.

The user's guidance on 29 October:

- Forced draft, or bottled or pressurized gas, needs a chimney only to a height that is safe for
  people. Natural draft follows Rule 5, tuned between the p.130 proportions (minimum) and Rule 5
  (maximum).
- A gas kiln's inlet (burner port) and outlet (exit flue) areas are about 1:1 (p.151, p.78,
  p.236, p.174).

## What changes in the engine

1. **Gas chimney.** Pressurized gas (propane at pounds, power burners): chimney as tall as the
   kiln or its top framework, then a hood and stack (p.151). Natural draft: Rule 5 (p.80) as the
   most, the p.130 proportions as the least, with Olsen's allowances (forced draft about ¼ off,
   taper, altitude). A design may give its chimney height and section; the engine checks them.
2. **Flues.** Exit flue = total inlet (burner-port) area, then restricted (p.151); updrafts up to
   40 ft³ equal or slightly under, larger ones more (p.173–174); natural draft inlet = exit =
   chimney section (p.78). 2½ in²/ft³ stays as one of his three methods, not the requirement.
3. **Burner input.** Olsen's Rule 1 (IFB 10,000, dense 16,000–19,000 BTU/ft³/hr, p.226) beside
   Ward's table. The error is physical: the burners' available heat at top temperature must cover
   the steady heat loss there. Below the rule of thumb is a slower firing (p.226: 6,000 BTU/ft³
   fired cone 10 in 12–15 h), a note, not a failure.
4. **Fuels and burners.** Natural gas from a line; burners rated by the design (BTU/hr each) when
   they aren't in the library; floor burners for updrafts; more than 8 burners (Kaneko's 32).
5. **Size.** Gas kilns over 60 ft³ are no longer refused: the book's rules cover 12–500 ft³.
6. **Arches.** Rise from Olsen: 1½ in/ft and up (featheredge), no fixed maximum (he builds
   4–6 in/ft; p.51, p.296); a steep arch is checked by Heyman's thickness factor instead.
7. **Altitude.** Site elevation: burner output, flue and chimney allowances (p.81–82, p.174,
   p.228–232).
8. **Electric.** Surface load from Kanthal's chart as Olsen prints it (6.4–9 W/in² near cone 10,
   p.253, p.257) in place of Krueger's 17.5; power density against Table 9-6; the worked
   26 kW example (p.258–260) as a validation case.
9. **Wood, coal and oil.** Fireboxes: grate, ash pit, inlet flues, stoke holes; grate against
   chimney (7–10 : 1; groundhogs about 4 : 1), coal grate per floor area, oil by GPH.
10. **Shapes.** Round chambers (bottle, Singh), tube and tunnel kilns on a slope, and climbing
    chamber kilns. Where the 3D model or plans can't draw a shape yet, the build says so (a note,
    not an error); the calculations still run.
11. **Materials.** K-25, K-26 and JM 30 from Morgan's 2025 sheets (done). Others only with a
    source.
