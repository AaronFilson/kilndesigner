## The user's guidance (30 October)

Recorded in `docs/research/kiln-reference.md` and `electric-kiln-survey.md`, cited to the user:

- **Fireboxes depend on the firing wanted.** The most heat comes from a high grate with the most
  air (fast-fire kilns); some kilns send ash up round the pots for ash glazing and flashing, the
  firebox part of the first chamber. The engine gives options and shows the numbers, and warns
  only on what is physically wrong.
- **Round walls.** No 4½ in circle-brick table exists, and round face brick is little used.
  Rings are straights with mortar-filled wedge joints (Olsen Fig. 2-13, p.45), straights with
  featheredges, or straights cut to the angle, the norm in electric kilns. Cut rings are built in
  whole bricks, the full 9 in face outside: electric kilns are named 6, 8, 10, 12 bricks round.
  A set size takes the fewest bricks and a closer, allowed but not usual. Near enough is 1/16 in,
  dry-laid; mortared joints take more. In small kilns a smooth wall matters more than staggered
  joints. Longer bricks (13½ in) are seldom worth their cost.
- **Skutt's names:** KM-1227-3 is Kiln Master, 12 sides, 27 in inside, 3 in brick; no "-3" means
  2½ in; KMT is the touch-screen controller.

## Round 2 (30 October): done

All on `m6`, every check green (1531 tests, Python and web coverage over 98 %, all 22 Playwright
specs against a rebuilt compose stack).

1. **The web form** edits `chamber.rings`, a firebox's `step`, and a door opening (width, height,
   across, sill, head, `each_chamber`); fireboxes, chimneys, round chambers, open tops and floors
   on the ground were already in it. Playwright covers rings, a full firebox and an open-top kiln.
2. **Round electric kilns.** A cut ring of whole bricks makes the chamber a regular N-sided
   polygon (area, volume and heat loss from flats and corners); elements run in grooves round
   the facets, one per 4½ in course; the lid and floor slab are polygons of mortared straights,
   as many whole as fit, the edge bricks cut to the profile, each in a steel band, the lid's
   hinged (the user); a "round electric" template like the KM-1227-3. Unsourced and left out:
   groove pitch and depth, coil bend at a corner, band sizes, Skutt's brick grade.
3. **Doors in part of a wall** (`loading.opening`), laid out from every door in Olsen's figures
   (`door-openings.md`): none of his kilns uses a steel lintel; most have semicircular arched
   doorways on brick jambs. Checked: a steel lintel hot (AISC F10, stress, deflection); an arched
   head by Heyman's thrust-line statics (`frame.door-arch`: twice the thinnest standing ring, a
   thrust line inside it) and its jambs as buttresses (`frame.door-jamb`: the base resultant
   inside, then in the middle third). Bidai and Kawaii now have a door in each chamber's side
   wall, as built, and pass. **Bearing stress** (`refractory-bearing.md`): makers' cold
   crushing strengths and hot-load sag are recorded, but no source gives an allowable stress or
   factor of safety for refractory, so bearing is shown, not checked.

## The user's guidance (30 October, later)

- **Electric kiln depth:** Paragon's 22¼ in is 27 − 4½ in: the walls run to the bottom and the
  floor sits inside them. Skutt's KM-1227-3 has 27 in inside, its floor below the walls. Both
  builds exist (`electric-kiln-survey.md`).
- **Unclear figures:** where Olsen's imperial figure is unreadable, look for the metric value
  printed with it. Done for the doors: Tamba, Bowen Mountain and Kawaii print no metric door size,
  so none is settled; the p.93 bundle (15 in, 38 cm) is. About 240 other unclear marks are still
  to check.
- **Complex curves:** shapes by choice first, point editing later (the user). Plan support for Calimpong and the other cast or conical-brick kilns:
  [complex-curves.md](complex-curves.md).
