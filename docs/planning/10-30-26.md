## The user's guidance (30 October)

Recorded in `docs/research/kiln-reference.md` and `electric-kiln-survey.md`, cited to the user:

- **Fireboxes depend on the firing wanted.** The most heat comes from a high grate with the most
  air (fast-fire kilns); some kilns send ash up round the pots for ash glazing and flashing, the
  firebox part of the first chamber. The engine gives options and shows the numbers, and warns
  only on what is physically wrong.
- **Round walls.** No 4½ in circle-brick table exists, and round face brick is little used.
  Rings are straights with mortar-filled wedge joints (Olsen Fig. 2-13, p.45), straights with
  featheredges, or straights cut to the angle, the norm in electric kilns. Cut rings are built in
  whole bricks, the full 9 in face outside: electric kilns are named 6, 8, 10, 12 bricks round.
  A set size takes the fewest bricks and a closer, allowed but not usual. Near enough is 1/16 in,
  dry-laid; mortared joints take more. In small kilns a smooth wall matters more than staggered
  joints. Longer bricks (13½ in) are seldom worth their cost.
- **Skutt's names:** KM-1227-3 is Kiln Master, 12 sides, 27 in inside, 3 in brick; no "-3" means
  2½ in; KMT is the touch-screen controller.

## The next round, in order

1. **The web form for the new choices.** `chamber.rings`, a firebox's `step`, `ash_pit` and grate,
   and the long-standing gaps: fireboxes, chimneys, round chambers, open tops and floors on the
   ground are YAML-only. Each gets a form control and a Playwright check.
2. **Round electric kilns, finished.** The cut ring is laid; the rest of the kiln isn't:
   - elements in grooves on a faceted wall (the element design assumes a box's walls);
   - the lid and floor slabs of a round electric kiln, and the steel bands round each section
     (Skutt's repair guide loosens "the steel band"; no source sizes one yet);
   - a design started from "round electric", with 8, 10 and 12 sides of 2½ or 3 in brick;
   - more models in the spec log (Paragon, L&L JD, Olympic, Evenheat, Bailey) and Skutt's parts
     list read as text, to check course count and bricks per ring.
3. **Doors in part of a wall.** The design can only make a whole wall a door. A door with its
   width, height and position lets the lintel be checked (method in `steel-design.md` §S5) and
   frames be placed for round, row and sloping kilns. Blocked part: a safe bearing stress for a
   steel lintel's ends on refractory brick is not found.
4. **The author's kiln, end to end.** Rebuild `pnw-hybrid-crossdraft` on today's engine, read its
   report against Olsen, and give its castable roof a sourced specific heat so the firing
   simulation runs without a user value.
5. **M7, the client's kiln.** Needs the client's answers (open question 3). Wood firing
   performance (air to the coals, burn rate, ash carried) needs a combustion source for wood; none
   is in `docs/research/` yet.
