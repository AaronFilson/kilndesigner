## A. Measure ports, spy holes and flues off the figures, taken as roughly to scale

The user's ruling (2026-11-09): Olsen's drawings are roughly to scale, so where the book prints
no size for a port, spy hole, flue, firebox or door, measure it off the figure.

- **Method.** Scale each figure from a dimension printed on it (a chamber width, a chimney
  height, a dimension line), never from another kiln. Where two printed dimensions exist, scale
  from both and report the spread; that spread is the reading's uncertainty. A figure with no
  printed dimension can't be scaled: say so, don't scale from a brick module unless the
  figure shows countable courses (then 2½ in per course, 9 in per stretcher, Olsen p.51).
- **Record.** In the YAML, `(scaled: Fig x-y, p.N, from <printed dim>, ±n in)`; in each kiln's
  doc, a column "scaled" beside "printed" and "drawing". Round to the brick module where the
  part is laid in brick (a spy hole is a half-brick or a brick out), and say when it was.
- **What.** Spy holes (count, size, height, which face), burner ports, exit and floor flues,
  flues between chambers, firebox mouths, stoke holes, ash pits, grate heights, dampers,
  door openings, chimney sections.
- **Precedence.** Printed number > scaled with a stated spread > "(drawing)" reading > left out.
  A scaled value never overrides a printed one; a disagreement of more than the spread is
  recorded as a question for the user.

## C. Engine: statics and movement (sixth round, proposed there) [done 2026-11-09: `movement.py`; 3 stays proposed]


1. **Expansion allowance in walls.** Growth 3/32 in per foot to 2400 °F (Olsen p.51; Brosnan in
   Schacht's *Refractories Handbook*), half of it to be left open (Bhatia, PDHonline M158 p.29;
   Olsen's 1/16 in per foot in a steel frame). A finding per wall run with the allowance and
   where it goes; Olsen's ¼ in fibre joints past 48 in; no expansion joint over an arch or near
   its springing (BIA).
2. **Joints thicker than ¼ in warned** (Beall, *Masonry Design and Detailing* 5th ed. p.143),
   where a design states a joint (wedge-joint rings, mortar).
3. **Arch rise on heating inside a held span**: keeping arc length (1+ε)L over a fixed span S,
   sin(θ'/2)/θ' = S/(2(1+ε)L). Needs a published source or a textbook check before it ships;
   until then, proposed only.
4. Permanent growth of bauxite, kyanite and andalusite past their firing temperature, and IFB
   shrinkage near its rating: notes on the materials, shown in the working where they apply.

## F. Firebox efficiency from the flue loss (proposed, `docs/research/firebox-efficiency.md` §6)

Not built. Integrate a wood firing step by step as propane is: η_step = 1 − q(T_chamber, λ) − u,
q the flue gas's sensible heat (Cantera, wood as 50/6/44 C/H/O at the user's moisture), λ the
user's air ratio, u an unburnt allowance (no source yet). Derived flue loss: 25–29 % at 600 °C,
60–69 % at 1300 °C for λ 1.0–1.2. Before building: read Pettersen (1984) at the page for the
composition, and check against one weighed firing. Until then `firebox_efficiency` stays the
user's, starting range 15–30 %.

Questions for the user: do you have, or can you get, the weight of wood for one firing of a known
kiln (any wood kiln you've fired), and an O₂ or CO₂ reading at the flue? Either would test the
range and the formula.

   radiation between faces goes into engine v2's zone model only (ADR 0017, decision 10; §H).
3. **Hot emissivities: found in part (radiation-exchange.md §5).**
   - **Dense fire brick:** 0.75–0.79 (low alumina) or 0.55–0.63 (medium) at 800–1200 °C (Osborn
     1985). Sparrow and Cess's fireclay brick, 0.75 at 980 °C, agrees.
   - **IFB:** 0.37–0.64 over 790–1077 °C, varying by grade (Morgan's MPK grades, Hampartsoumian
     1990).
   - **Fibre board:** 0.48–0.78 (Fletcher and Williams 1984).

   Still missing:
   - Osborn's alumina classes (which one a kiln's brick falls in);
   - IFB above 1077 °C;
   - the chemistry of K-23 and K-26 IFB, to match them to a measured grade;
   - castables, kiln wash and glaze.

   **Decided (the user, 2026-11-09): extrapolate** past the measured range, flag it in the
   working, and search for more IFB data later (`02-plan.md`, follow-up research). K-26 and
   JM 26 are classified to 1430 °C, well past cone 10. The same rule is applied to fibre board.
4. **Gas radiation:** the method is sourced (Lienhard §10.5, Hottel's framework with Alberti et
   al.'s charts; radiation-exchange.md §6). Its correlations are not yet in hand.

**The reference kilns are working examples (the user, 2026-11-10, before the overnight run):**
"the kilns in the reference material all worked for the people that built them for the purpose
they were built for. Those are the starting point, and the model has to let them stand as working
examples. Formulas then go to get the right info for the engine, and existing modules are used in
the right places in combo with the right bridges." So:
- "Every reference kiln stands" is a test target for v2's statics.
- Where v2 says a reference kiln can't stand (an arch with no maximum-thrust state, a frame past
  yield, a dome or vault with no admissible state), that is a fault in the model, not a verdict on
  the kiln. The fix is the missing, sourced physics (hot crushing through the C16 stand-in, creep,
  joint closure, the frame's give, springings that move, better force patterns), never a
  parameter fitted to the kiln, and never a flag on it.
- Where the target can't be met yet, the missing physics is recorded as an open item, not as a
  failing verdict.

**v2 statics, built in the overnight run of 2026-11-10** (`core/src/kilndesigner/v2/statics/`,
tests in `core/tests/v2/test_statics_*.py`):
1. **Blocks and contacts** (`blocks.py`): rigid blocks from build123d solids; contacts by the
   pairwise planar method and OpenCascade's General Fuse for curved faces.
2. **The rigid-block LP** (`rigid.py`, `arch.py`): equilibrium, no tension, Coulomb friction,
   crushing stress blocks, a load factor, Heyman's geometric factor; a semicircle's least
   thickness 0.107478 (published 0.1075), N1 reproduced; the least and most thrust equal the
   closed forms of `arch-thrust-theory.md` §3.
3. **The kiln's cross-section** (`section.py`): walls in 2½ in courses, skewbacks and voussoirs cut
   from the engine's own solids, with the frame, a buttress or nothing; the least force on what
   holds the arch, the most (the hot cap), and each bed joint's resultant and friction (decision
   7). The slice moves along the run to clear openings in the walls. 31 of the 53 reference kilns
   are sprung arches across the width: every one stands cold and hot. N3 falls; N4 restated
   (`engine-v2-negative-cases.md` §7).
   - Found: with the 10 psi C16 stand-in the fast-fire arch's most thrust is 170 lbf/ft (176 in
     the section), not the probe's 80: the flat-line cap left out the ring's own weight, which
     sags the line as much as the ring's 0.6 in of room.
4. **Non-associative friction** (`nonassoc.py`): HiGHS's MILP over each joint point's mode
   (open, sticking, sliding either way), Ferris and Tin-Loi's least collapse load; the survey's
   case C reproduced (2.24546 against the LP's 2.93941 kN/m, proven in 0.04 s). On a kiln:
   each wall alone under the arch's push (`section.wall_capacity`), what it takes with and
   without associative friction, and what the frame or buttress must still give. One column of
   courses is statically determinate (the two agree); two layers side by side differ where their
   collar joint slides (kit-24: 7.7 against 8.4 lbf/ft, not proven least in 60 s with 264
   binaries). Hot, the check leaves out the crushing stand-in: a crushing hinge isn't one of the
   modes.
5. **The frame** (`frame.py`, PyNiteFEA 3.2.1 added to the engine): the buttress angles, posts
   and rods in PyNite, E and Fy at the steel's temperature by AISC 360-16 Table A-4.2.1 (refused
   past 1,400 °F), the flexibility at the slices by unit loads (0.015 % from the hand formula);
   the arch–frame compatibility as bounded least squares between the section's cold least and hot
   most push; the angle's moment and the rods' pull from PyNite under the solved pushes. The
   probe's 4-slice case is reproduced (580.5 / 207.8 lbf/ft, 21.6 ksi). **The slicing now
   converges**: the C16 stand-in caps every slice, so the fast-fire arch with cold steel reaches
   its 176 lbf/ft cap all along the bay and the L2×2×3/16 angle sits at 12.6 ksi (A36 yields at
   36; HarbisonWalker's limit for hot bindings is 12), converged from 4 to 8 slices. The rods'
   own growth at 600 °F gives the whole 0.5 % back. The frame is an input: the design files
   carry none, so the reference run takes the book's where printed (fast-fire, p.139) and v1's
   sizing, marked assumed, elsewhere. The arch's growth is its material's band (IFB 0.5–0.8 %,
   fireclay 3/64–3/32 in/ft), the ring at its hot face's temperature until the heat model gives
   its mean; the steel's temperature an input (70 °F, the cautious end) until then.
