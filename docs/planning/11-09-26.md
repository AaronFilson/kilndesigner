## A. Measure ports, spy holes and flues off the figures, taken as roughly to scale

The user's ruling (2026-11-09): Olsen's drawings are roughly to scale, so where the book prints
no size for a port, spy hole, flue, firebox or door, measure it off the figure.

- **Method.** Scale each figure from a dimension printed on it (a chamber width, a chimney
  height, a dimension line), never from another kiln. Where two printed dimensions exist, scale
  from both and report the spread; that spread is the reading's uncertainty. A figure with no
  printed dimension can't be scaled: say so, don't scale from a brick module unless the
  figure shows countable courses (then 2½ in per course, 9 in per stretcher, Olsen p.51).
- **Record.** In the YAML, `(scaled: Fig x-y, p.N, from <printed dim>, ±n in)`; in each kiln's
  doc, a column "scaled" beside "printed" and "drawing". Round to the brick module where the
  part is laid in brick (a spy hole is a half-brick or a brick out), and say when it was.
- **What.** Spy holes (count, size, height, which face), burner ports, exit and floor flues,
  flues between chambers, firebox mouths, stoke holes, ash pits, grate heights, dampers,
  door openings, chimney sections.
- **Precedence.** Printed number > scaled with a stated spread > "(drawing)" reading > left out.
  A scaled value never overrides a printed one; a disagreement of more than the spread is
  recorded as a question for the user.

## C. Engine: statics and movement (sixth round, proposed there) [done 2026-11-09: `movement.py`; 3 stays proposed]


1. **Expansion allowance in walls.** Growth 3/32 in per foot to 2400 °F (Olsen p.51; Brosnan in
   Schacht's *Refractories Handbook*), half of it to be left open (Bhatia, PDHonline M158 p.29;
   Olsen's 1/16 in per foot in a steel frame). A finding per wall run with the allowance and
   where it goes; Olsen's ¼ in fibre joints past 48 in; no expansion joint over an arch or near
   its springing (BIA).
2. **Joints thicker than ¼ in warned** (Beall, *Masonry Design and Detailing* 5th ed. p.143),
   where a design states a joint (wedge-joint rings, mortar).
3. **Arch rise on heating inside a held span**: keeping arc length (1+ε)L over a fixed span S,
   sin(θ'/2)/θ' = S/(2(1+ε)L). Needs a published source or a textbook check before it ships;
   until then, proposed only.
4. Permanent growth of bauxite, kyanite and andalusite past their firing temperature, and IFB
   shrinkage near its rating: notes on the materials, shown in the working where they apply.

## F. Firebox efficiency from the flue loss (proposed, `docs/research/firebox-efficiency.md` §6)

Not built. Integrate a wood firing step by step as propane is: η_step = 1 − q(T_chamber, λ) − u,
q the flue gas's sensible heat (Cantera, wood as 50/6/44 C/H/O at the user's moisture), λ the
user's air ratio, u an unburnt allowance (no source yet). Derived flue loss: 25–29 % at 600 °C,
60–69 % at 1300 °C for λ 1.0–1.2. Before building: read Pettersen (1984) at the page for the
composition, and check against one weighed firing. Until then `firebox_efficiency` stays the
user's, starting range 15–30 %.

Questions for the user: do you have, or can you get, the weight of wood for one firing of a known
kiln (any wood kiln you've fired), and an O₂ or CO₂ reading at the flue? Either would test the
range and the formula.

Notes for E from `docs/research/heat-libraries.md` (ht 1.2.0 and heatrapy 2.1.1, both MIT):

- Hot face down: our 0.27 Ra^¼ is McAdams's and the highest of five forms. VDI's 0.6 Ra^⅕ and
  Raithby–Hollands' Ra^⅕ form (Rohsenow et al. 1998), both at our L = A/P, give 25–30 % and
  32–38 % less on a 0.9–1.8 m floor at 60–250 °C; C1340 (L = √A) ~50 % less. Combined h on that
  face −9 %. Provisional verdict: move to Raithby–Hollands once its Ra range is read.
- Hot face up: VDI gives ~35 % more than Lloyd–Moran at roof sizes; quote as uncertainty.
- Chimneys: Popiel–Churchill's cylinder correction is laminar only (Ra < 10⁹); real stacks are
  at ~10¹¹; the plate is within 3 %. Add the D/H ≥ 35/Gr^¼ slender-stack check.
- c_p(T) and k(T) of generic refractories at 400–1200 °C: VDI Heat Atlas table via
  `ht.insulation.refractories`; its L1260/L1400 k agree with Morgan JM 23/26 within 11 %.
  ht clamps outside the range; we would refuse.
- ht has no view factors and no wall-corner or floor shape factors.
- heatrapy's Crank–Nicolson schemes use no or arithmetic-mean interface k and have no
  analytical validation; our backward Euler with harmonic interfaces is better. Tests to add:
  two-layer composite slab (de Monte 2000), convective-face plane wall (Incropera §5.5),
  linear surface ramp (Carslaw & Jaeger).
