## A. Measure ports, spy holes and flues off the figures, taken as roughly to scale

The user's ruling (2026-11-09): Olsen's drawings are roughly to scale, so where the book prints
no size for a port, spy hole, flue, firebox or door, measure it off the figure.

- **Method.** Scale each figure from a dimension printed on it (a chamber width, a chimney
  height, a dimension line), never from another kiln. Where two printed dimensions exist, scale
  from both and report the spread; that spread is the reading's uncertainty. A figure with no
  printed dimension can't be scaled: say so, don't scale from a brick module unless the
  figure shows countable courses (then 2½ in per course, 9 in per stretcher, Olsen p.51).
- **Record.** In the YAML, `(scaled: Fig x-y, p.N, from <printed dim>, ±n in)`; in each kiln's
  doc, a column "scaled" beside "printed" and "drawing". Round to the brick module where the
  part is laid in brick (a spy hole is a half-brick or a brick out), and say when it was.
- **What.** Spy holes (count, size, height, which face), burner ports, exit and floor flues,
  flues between chambers, firebox mouths, stoke holes, ash pits, grate heights, dampers,
  door openings, chimney sections.
- **Precedence.** Printed number > scaled with a stated spread > "(drawing)" reading > left out.
  A scaled value never overrides a printed one; a disagreement of more than the spread is
  recorded as a question for the user.

## C. Engine: statics and movement (sixth round, proposed there) [done 2026-11-09: `movement.py`; 3 stays proposed]


1. **Expansion allowance in walls.** Growth 3/32 in per foot to 2400 °F (Olsen p.51; Brosnan in
   Schacht's *Refractories Handbook*), half of it to be left open (Bhatia, PDHonline M158 p.29;
   Olsen's 1/16 in per foot in a steel frame). A finding per wall run with the allowance and
   where it goes; Olsen's ¼ in fibre joints past 48 in; no expansion joint over an arch or near
   its springing (BIA).
2. **Joints thicker than ¼ in warned** (Beall, *Masonry Design and Detailing* 5th ed. p.143),
   where a design states a joint (wedge-joint rings, mortar).
3. **Arch rise on heating inside a held span**: keeping arc length (1+ε)L over a fixed span S,
   sin(θ'/2)/θ' = S/(2(1+ε)L). Needs a published source or a textbook check before it ships;
   until then, proposed only.
4. Permanent growth of bauxite, kyanite and andalusite past their firing temperature, and IFB
   shrinkage near its rating: notes on the materials, shown in the working where they apply.

## F. Firebox efficiency from the flue loss (proposed, `docs/research/firebox-efficiency.md` §6)

Not built. Integrate a wood firing step by step as propane is: η_step = 1 − q(T_chamber, λ) − u,

60–69 % at 1300 °C for λ 1.0–1.2. Before building: read Pettersen (1984) at the page for the
composition, and check against one weighed firing. Until then `firebox_efficiency` stays the
user's, starting range 15–30 %.

### Step 0: the reference firings and the must-fail case

file's lining (4½ in medium-duty over 4½ in K-23, assumed) passes the same check by 5.6 kW: a
necessary condition only.

### Step 1: the gas (`heat/gas.py`)

Each fuel's products at equilibrium at λ, tabulated 290–3000 K at 10 K (enthalpy, ρ, μ, k, c_p,
x_H₂O, x_CO₂, x_CO), and the heat a zone keeps when its gas leaves at T, q(T) = H_reactants −
m·h_eq(T) per kg of fuel (of oven-dry wood for wood). Gas fuels are their Cantera species, as v1;
wood is the 50/6/44 surrogate with its enthalpy set by its net heating value and its water entering
liquid (FAO eq. 3), so Cantera supplies only the products. Refused: λ below 0.8, temperatures
outside 300–3000 K, wood outside 0–50 % moisture or 15–23 MJ/kg. Checks (60 tests): v1's
`available_heat` to 1e-4 (propane, methane; λ 0.9–1.5; 400–1904 K); methane's net heat, NIST less
the water (50.04 MJ/kg); FAO's wood formula to 1e-4; 5.89 kg of air per kg of dry wood; the
review's wood flue-loss table (λ 0.8–2.0, 200–1300 °C) to 0.1 point; equilibrium CO in reduction;
propane flue-gas transport (review §7). Still open: Pettersen (1984) read at the page; coal and oil
compositions (no source: refused).

### Step 2: the draft (`heat/draft.py`)

The pressure balance round the gas path, Σ(ρ_room − ρ_gas)gΔz = ΣKρv²/2, solved for the air:
fluids for friction and fittings, the gas tables for the gas. Each hot element carries its zone
("firebox", "chamber", "chimney"), so zones in series can give each its own temperature. Built
from a design: under-floor stoked fireboxes (ash-pit mouth, grate, firebox, inlet flues) or gas
burners' ports, the chamber, one exit flue, the run, one chimney; the site's elevation through the
US Standard Atmosphere 1976 (fluids). A draft table gives the most air (damper open) against the gas
temperature. Stated in the working: round-pipe fittings on brick flues through area-equivalent
diameters (Idelchik's rectangular forms still *pending*); brick roughness a band, smooth to
fluids' 'Rough concrete' (3.05 mm), drawn at its rough end; the fuel bed's and setting's resistance
left out (an upper bound on the air); a burner kiln's venturi pumping not modelled. Refused: no
chimney or exit flue in the design (the rule-sized defaults aren't a kiln's own draft), several
exits or chimneys, fireboxes against a wall, a chimney through the crown, floor burners.

Checks (17 tests): Bernoulli's closed form for a lone chimney; the survey's fast-fire probe on its
own inputs to 0.2 % at 350–1600 K (0.0761, 0.1059, 0.0969, 0.0822 kg/s); thinner air at 6000 ft
draws under 90 %. The fast-fire from its own design file (the grate's ¾ in bars from p.204): 0.105
kg/s at its most near 330 °C, 0.082 kg/s at 1327 °C (2–3 % more at a smooth wall), 50 kg/h of dry
wood at λ 1 there; the turn up the chimney is the largest loss (14 Pa of 43).
**The 36 ft³ cross-draft's draft can't be worked out**: its design gives no chimney or flue.

### Step 3: rays (`heat/rays.py`, `heat/chamber.py`)

Monte Carlo radiation between faces on trimesh + Embree: points uniform by area and cosine
directions from every zone, first hits tallied and each pair's path lengths kept as 200 quantiles,
so a grey gas of any k needs no new rays (s_i s_j(k), g s_i(k), Hottel's zone method with one gas
zone). The chamber comes from the engine's own solid (`massing._cavity`), faces named by place, the
end walls' segments over the springline with their walls; the openings are rectangles carved from
their faces: the exit flue (design), the under-floor inlet flues (the engine's layout,
`firebox_parts.openings`), burner ports (spread as the engine lays them) and spy holes; the setting
a box in the way. One straight box chamber only (round chambers, rows, bends wait for step 9).

**The closure** (summation and reciprocity to round-off, as `radiation.Enclosure` demands): each
pair's two estimates combined by their variances (Loehrke et al. 1995, via Frangi 2025 §3.5.4),
then Larsen & Howell's least change weighted by the combined variance. Found in the run: with a
plain average of the two directions (the survey's "symmetric average, then smoothing"), the 2½ in
spy hole's row moved by 0.011 at 2×10⁵ rays, a large face's few hits on a small opening spoiling
the opening's own precise estimate; with the variance weighting the closure moves every factor by
the noise only (0.0012 at 2×10⁵ rays).

Checks (23 tests): Embree is the backend; the unit cube against Howell C-11/C-14 within 3e-3 at 10⁵
rays a face; Cauchy's 4V = Σ A_i·mean chord to 0.5 %; exact closure for k 0–3 m⁻¹; a sphere's
grey-gas absorptance against its closed form (kD 0.1, 1, 3) within 4e-3; a half shadow (0.0999 of
0.1998); the engine's sprung arch against `radiation.covered` (the crown's area to 0.2 %, factors
within 4e-3); the fast-fire's own chamber with its four openings and a setting, left and right
symmetric. **A layout fault found**: the engine's layout of the fast-fire's under-floor fireboxes
puts each 4½ × 13½ in inlet flue 114 mm under a side wall; the heat model moves it onto the floor at
its printed size and says so. (The B2 note already has the fast-fire's two fireboxes laid out side
by side across the width, where Olsen's run along the front and back walls in an S, p.140, p.204.)

### Step 4: gas radiation (`heat/gasrad.py`)

Bordbar 2014's WSGG set (168 coefficients as NIST FDS and RadLib code them; one continuous fit in
MR 0.01–4, 300–2400 K) as the adopted set, Dorigon 2013 (MR 1 and 2, ENCIT 2012 Tables 1–2) for the
band; the zone method with one gas zone summed over the grey gases and the clear one, on the ray
trace's exchange areas. Outside a set's temperature range its weights are held at the ends (as FDS
does; a surface below 300 K emits under 0.2 % of what it does at 1000 K), said in the working.
Checks (16 tests): Lienhard Ex. 10.11 (CO₂ 0.2 atm, 40 cm, 1200 °C; chart 0.085) gives 0.0866 with
Bordbar at MR 0.01; Bordbar reads −0.4 % to +13.3 % against Dorigon at MR 1 and 2 (L 0.1–3 m,
600–1600 K), the band; weights positive and summing under 1 over 400–2400 K; a transparent gas
reproduces `radiation.exchange` (Lienhard eq. 10.42) to 1e-12; black isothermal walls exchange
only with the gas; the gas loses exactly what the surfaces gain. Soot is not counted (no kiln
flame's soot fraction in any source read; survey question 3).

### Step 5: walls (`heat/walls.py`)

`TransientWall`'s physics (k(T), c_p(T), harmonic interfaces, area growing with depth, 5 mm cells)
as rates for the DAE, nothing lagged; the hot and outer faces algebraic; the outside still air
(v1's `StillAirSurface`), adiabatic, or a ground. Each face's outside area is its inside area grown
as its bounding rectangle grows by its thickness (the six rectangles tile the outer box). Checks
on IDA (6 tests): the semi-infinite solid (erf, and the heat taken in, 2kΔT√(t/πα)) within 1 %;
Incropera §5.5's convective plane wall (Bi 2, Fo 0.5, the series) within 0.003 in θ; v1's
`TransientWall` on a 10 h ramp of a two-layer lining within 1 K; held 2,000 h, v1's steady
`solve_wall` (k(T) integrated exactly, ASTM C680's way) within 1 %. De Monte's two-layer series
wasn't coded; the two-layer lining is checked against `TransientWall` and `solve_wall` instead.

### Step 6: zones and the firing on IDA (`heat/zone.py`, `heat/firing.py`, `heat/kiln.py`)

One well-stirred gas zone as a DAE on SUNDIALS IDA (sparse Jacobian pattern): the gas holds no
heat and leaves at its own temperature, F·q(T_g) = Σ walls + setting + openings; each wall's hot
face balances radiation and convection against its first cell; the setting is one lumped node on
v1's fired-clay curve; the zone method over the rays' exchange areas with Bordbar's grey gases.
Openings: spy holes black at the room's temperature (H13), inlet flues and burner ports black at the
gas's (the fire seen through them, inside its control volume), the exit flue at its wall's hot
face (its channel's brick; the chimney's walls as their own zone wait for step 9). Modes: max fire
(a stoked kiln at the air its draft draws, damper open), fixed input (burners at their rating), a
schedule held by the gas or by the setting, restarted at each break. The setting's temperatures
are IDA events. `kiln.py` builds the model from a design: the fuel, the engine's chamber and
openings, the rays with the setting's box, the walls from the lining (hot-face emissivity lines:
Morgan MPK 125 for IFB, Osborn's medium-alumina for dense brick, ×0.95 hemispherical, extrapolated
by the user's rule), the shell's emissivity (CIBSE, v1's), the draft table where the path builds.
**Stated stand-ins**, in the working: inside convection from ht's laminar flat plate at the mean
velocity (no kiln correlation is sourced); a floor over under-floor fireboxes adiabatic (the
fireboxes aren't zones yet); IFB c_p held at its one sourced point, 1.21 kJ/(kg·K) (the library's
rule); dense brick from fired clay's 800 J/(kg·K) at 24 °C up to VDI's points (v1's ware curve).
The load is required (ADR 0017 decision 11); the reference loads are test inputs, said.

Checks (`test_heat_firing.py`, 12 tests): the energy balance closes to round-off over a firing
(fuel's net heat = flue + walls + setting + openings, 1e-9); IDA converged (cone 10 within 0.2 %
between rtol 1e-5 and 1e-7); each mode; refusals (max fire with no draft, an impossible start, a
gas past the tables, coal, elements, wood with no spec, a setting outside the chamber, a material
with no hot emissivity or c_p).

### Step 7: the ground (`heat/ground.py`)

scikit-fem's axisymmetric heat equation (P1, r-weighted forms) in the soil under a grounded floor
(the design's `chamber.on_ground` or a foundation, as v1 reads it): the floor as the disc of its
outside area, the soil round it adiabatic (Incropera case 10's assumption), the far sides 8 m off
at the room's temperature; dry mineral soil (Lienhard Table A.2), damp clay (DIN EN 12524 via ht)
the band's other end. The mass is lumped, so the disc's own soil becomes the floor's outer face's
capacity (that face turns differential) and the soil's free nodes join the DAE with their
stiffness's sparsity. Checks (6 tests): the survey's 2-D step response (its 1,548-node mesh,
consistent mass) within 1.5 % from 15 min to 30 days on 700 nodes; above the slab early, the steady
disc late (2.565 m against the survey's 2.541 and Incropera's half-space 2.400 for the finite
domain); the DAE's rows vanish at steady state. Coupled: behind the cross-draft's 9 in floor the
soil changes nothing in 15 h (the heat front hasn't reached it, as the survey found for 4 h); with
a 2½ in dense floor the setting ends 62 K hotter on the ground than on a stand at 20 h (dry soil
loses less than a hot underside in still air over those hours). Not done: the ground round the
kiln losing to the air (the survey's h 10 was a test value; no source), k(T) of hot, drying soil.

### Step 9: zones in series (`heat/series.py`)

Well-stirred zones along the gas path, the gas leaving each at its own temperature into the next;
the fuel burns in the first, zone k keeping F·[q(T_k) − q(T_{k−1})]; one DAE over all the zones'
unknowns, each zone's rows its single-zone rows (`zone.Balance`, now told the gas coming in and the
other zones' temperatures). The flues between zones are black openings seeing the neighbour's
gas, as an inlet flue sees the fire; the gas-to-gas exchange of the zone method needs rays
starting in the gas volumes (not written), so what a zone sends through a link and the next
receives needn't match: the working gives the mismatch. Fixed input, or a schedule held by the
first zone's gas or setting. Checks (4 tests): one zone alone is `firing.fire`'s zone (to 0.05
K); two of W15's IFB cubes in a row on its ten burners: the second's gas cooler than the first's
throughout, the balance closed to round-off, the first's setting at cone 10 at 5.48 h, the
second's at 900 °C at 4.26 h; the links' mismatch −1.6 % of the fuel's heat. **Not built**: the
engine's rows of chambers mapped into it (`massing.cells` per chamber and `draft.between`'s flues),
so the multi-chamber stage times (Suzuki p.93) aren't run; fireboxes as zones; tube segments.
